## Body

### The short version

Applied Materials, which makes the machines used to make computer chips, reported its results for the
three months to 25 October 2026, the last quarter of its financial year, after the market closed on 12 November. In
short:

- **Revenue:** $10,409 million, a record, up 53% from $6,800 million a year earlier.
- **Chip-making machines:** revenue of $7,996 million, up 64%, driven by demand for chips for artificial
  intelligence.
- **Profit:** earnings per share of $3.40, up 43%. On Applied's own adjusted measure, $4.16, up 92%.
- **The forecast:** revenue of $10.6 billion to $11.6 billion in the next quarter, up 58% at the middle.
- **Next year:** Applied expects spending on chip-making equipment across the industry to rise more than 30% in
  2027.
- **The shares:** down 2.7% on 13 November, to $370.50, after falling 3.6% on 12 November before the results.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10,409M | $6,800M | +53% |
| Gross margin | 50.4% | 48.0% | +2.4 points |
| Operating margin | 35.1% | 25.2% | +9.9 points |
| Net income | $2,718M | $1,897M | +43% |
| Earnings per share | $3.40 | $2.38 | +43% |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $4.16 | $2.17 | +92% |

For the full financial year, revenue rose 21% to a record $34,446 million, and earnings per share rose 46% to
$12.62. Chief executive Gary Dickerson called it Applied's "seventh consecutive year of growth".

### Why profit rose 43% on one measure and 92% on the other

The gap is mostly Applied's investments in other companies, which it values at their share prices. This quarter it
recorded an unrealised loss of $653 million on them, which counts against reported profit. A year earlier it had
recorded a gain of $467 million, which helped reported profit.

Applied's adjusted measure leaves those out. So reported earnings per share grew from a base flattered by a gain, to
a figure held down by a loss, while the adjusted figure, which leaves out both, rose 92%. Its investments include
BE Semiconductor Industries, or Besi, a maker of chip-assembly machines listed in Amsterdam. On 26 October Applied
agreed to raise its stake to about 20%.

### Where the revenue came from

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chip-making machines | $7,996M | $4,879M | +64% |
| Services and parts | $1,896M | $1,506M | +26% |
| Other, mainly display | $517M | $415M | +25% |

Of the chip-making machines, 58% went to makers of logic chips, 33% to makers of DRAM memory and 9% to makers of
flash memory. A year earlier the DRAM share was 28%. Finance chief Brice Hill said Applied had record revenue in DRAM
and in advanced packaging, the technology that joins chips together.

By country, Taiwan was the largest customer at 28% of revenue, then China at 25%, down from 29% a year earlier,
then Korea at 17%. Hill said China would probably be "less than 25% of the business" in 2027.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts had expected adjusted earnings per share of $4.06 and revenue of $10.3 billion, Trefis reported. Applied
reported $4.16 and $10,409 million. Its own forecast in August had been revenue of $9.75 billion to $10.75 billion
and adjusted earnings per share of $3.82 to $4.22.

### The forecast

For the quarter to January 2027, Applied expects:

- revenue of $11.1 billion, give or take $500 million, which Hill said is up 58% at the middle;
- adjusted earnings per share of $4.35, give or take $0.20, which he said is up 83%;
- revenue from chip-making machines of about $8.7 billion, up 69%, Hill said.

Hill described it as a 14-week quarter, a week longer than usual.

Dickerson said Applied expects 2027 "to be another record year for the market and for Applied", with spending on
chip-making equipment "up more than 30% year-over-year". He said the pace would depend on how quickly the industry
can scale "all the way from clean room construction to equipment components and raw material supply".

### Making more machines

Hill said Applied was "investing to be able to double our systems output from Q4 '26 levels by 2028". He said more
than 10 new chip-factory projects had been added to Applied's tracker for the third quarter running, and that
Applied was tracking "more than 20 fab ramps and line expansions in 2027 alone". Applied's largest customers, Dickerson
said, now give it rolling forecasts eight quarters ahead.

### Cash and buy-backs

Applied generated a record $3,300 million of cash from operations in the quarter. It spent $2,000 million buying
back its own shares, which Hill called "the largest quarterly share buyback in over 8 years", and $421 million on
dividends.

### How the shares reacted

Applied closed at $395.08 on 11 November. On 12 November, before the results came out, it fell 3.6% to $380.95. On
13 November it opened at $376.01, fell as low as $352.23, and closed at $370.50, down 2.7%. The S&P 500 fell 0.4%
and the Nasdaq Composite 0.9% the same day.

GuruFocus, writing on TradingView, said the reaction looked "tied to the wider chip selloff" rather than to Applied's
numbers.

That leaves the shares 48.8% below their record close of $723.00 on 30 June 2026, though still up 44.2% since the
end of 2025. On 5 November they closed at $319.25, 55.8% below the record.

### How Applied shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of Applied's results releases before this one, from February 2016 to August 2026. Applied reports
after the market closes, so we compared the close on the next day with the close on the day it reported.

- The middle move, up or down, was **3.9%**.
- 15 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and 2 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **14.1%** in August 2025.
- The largest rise was **13.8%** in May 2016.
- The shares rose after 20 of the 43 and fell after 23.
- The last results, in August 2026, sent the shares down **5.1%**.

So a 2.7% fall is a small move for Applied: 26 of the 43 earlier results moved the shares more.

### How far Applied has fallen before

Counting dividends, Applied's shares have fallen 55% or more nine times since 1980, the start of our price history,
including this year. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

The eight earlier falls all got back to their record. Six of them took less than two years from the record; the
one that began in 2000 took 17 years. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether revenue for the quarter to January lands within $10.6 billion to $11.6 billion.
- **2027.** Whether industry spending on chip-making equipment rises more than 30%, as Applied expects.
- **Supply.** Whether Applied and its suppliers can make machines fast enough, and how quickly new chip factories
  are built.
- **China.** Whether its share of revenue falls below 25% next year, as Hill expects.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Applied's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours. The share-price
history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
