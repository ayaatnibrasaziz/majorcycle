### The short version

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, sold shares to the public for the first time on 18 November 2026
and began trading on the Nasdaq as ANT the next day. In short:

- **The offering:** 980 million shares at $83.00, raising $81.3 billion, the largest initial public offering on
  record. It had planned to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78.
- **The first two days:** the shares opened at $111.50 and closed the first day at $97.17. On the second day they fell
  as low as $83.05, five cents above the offer price, and closed at $88.91.
- **How many shares exist:** 13,219,642,276 after the offering. The 980 million sold are about 7.4% of them.
- **The lock-up:** most of the rest cannot be sold yet. Anthropic's prospectus lists 23 dates on which more becomes
  free to sell, starting on the first day of trading.
- **The next date:** 15 December 2026, when up to 303 million more shares can be sold.

### What a lock-up is

Before a company lists, its staff, founders and investors already own most of its shares. A lock-up is an agreement
with the banks running the offering not to sell those shares for a set period, so that they do not all reach the
market at once. The US Securities and Exchange Commission's guide for investors says most lock-ups prevent insiders
from selling "for 180 days", and that "a company's stock price may drop in anticipation that locked up shares will be
sold into the market when the lockup ends". Anthropic's ends in stages, and the last of them runs to day 365.

The prospectus says about 15.5 billion shares are covered by lock-up agreements with the banks or by similar
agreements with Anthropic. That count includes shares still to be issued from staff options and share awards and from
convertible notes, which is why it is larger than the 13.2 billion shares that exist today.

### How that compares with what was sold

- **By 2 February 2027,** the five fixed dates add up to as many as 1,627 million shares, about 1.7 times the 980
  million sold in the offering.
- **Counting the 980 million already sold,** at most about 2.6 billion shares, about 20% of the 13.2 billion, could
  be traded by 2 February. That leaves out the banks' option to buy 147 million more and the 313 million
  private-placement shares described below, which have their own limits.
- **Before day 180,** about 3.7 billion shares covered by the 180-day agreements may become eligible for sale, the
  prospectus says.

### Who can sell, and how much

The prospectus sets a different timetable for each group of holders. The percentages are shares of what each holder
had locked up:

- **Non-founder directors, officers, staff and consultants.** Up to 10% from the first trading day, including the
  finance chief and chief accounting officer; up to a further 35% two trading days after the results for 2026; the
  rest at the March 2027 results or day 180.
- **Holders of about 2.7 billion shares.** Up to 6.0% on 15 December and again on 5 January; 7.0% on 19 January and
  again on 2 February; 12.5% after the results for 2026; 9.5% at each of the five steps after them; the rest at the
  March 2027 results or day 180.
- **Holders of more than 0.5% of the shares, about 8.0 billion in all.** Smaller steps over a year: 1.8% on 15 December and 5
  January, 2.1% on 19 January and 2 February, 3.75% after the results for 2026, 2.85% at each of the five steps after them,
  4.2% at the March 2027 results or day 180, then 5.6%, 7.0% and 6.3% in steps after later results. The rest at the
  September 2027 results or day 365.
- **The seven co-founders.** Each sold 3,378,378 shares in the offering itself, worth about $280 million each at the
  offer price. Of the rest, up to 29% from the March 2027 results or day 180, and the remainder at the September 2027
  results or day 365. Through a company they own together, they hold 50.1% of the votes.

The prospectus also says the agreements carry "a number of exceptions", that Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, on
behalf of the banks running the offering, may release locked-up shares "in whole or in part at any time", and that from day 195 (1 June 2027) some holders may
hedge up to 20% of their remaining locked-up shares, about 719 million shares in all.

### What has happened when lock-ups end

The best-known study, by Laura Field and Gordon Hanka in the Journal of Finance in 2001, looked at 1,948 lock-ups. It
found "little selling by insiders" while they were in force. When they ended, trading volume rose by 40% for good and
the shares fell 1.5% on average over the three days around the expiry, more where the company was backed by venture
capital. That is an average across many companies, not a forecast for Anthropic.

### The other side of the counter

Retail investors received about 15% of the shares, CNBC reported, citing people familiar with the offering, below the
roughly 20% Anthropic had expected. Several of the brokers that offered them treat a quick sale as "flipping": at
Robinhood, selling within 30 days can bar a customer from new offerings for 60 days, CNBC reported. Those rules are
the brokers' own, not Anthropic's.

### What comes next

- **15 December 2026.** The second release: up to 303 million shares.
- **The results for 2026.** Anthropic has not given a date. Two trading days after them, up to 1.7 billion shares
  become free to sell, the largest single release before the March 2027 results.
- **17 May 2027 and 18 November 2027.** Day 180 and day 365: the latest the two largest releases can come if results
  are later.

### What this cannot tell you

These are the most shares that can be sold on each date, taken from the prospectus. How many holders will sell, and
what that does to the price, cannot be known in advance. Directors, officers and large shareholders are also limited by
securities rules and the company's insider-trading policy. This article is general information and does not take
into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
