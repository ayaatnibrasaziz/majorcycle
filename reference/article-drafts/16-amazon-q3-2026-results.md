## Body

### The short version

Amazon reported its results for the three months to 30 September after the US market closed on 29 October
2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $201.9 billion, up 12% from $180.2 billion a year earlier. Amazon says moving Prime Day into the
  second quarter took about 4 points off the growth rate.
- **AWS:** $46.0 billion, up 39%, which Amazon calls "its fastest growth in nearly five years".
- **Profit:** operating income of $28.1 billion, up from $17.4 billion, and an operating margin of 13.9%,
  "Amazon's highest operating margin ever".
- **Spending:** $77.3 billion on property and equipment in the quarter, more than double the $35.1 billion a
  year earlier.
- **The shares:** down 1.4% the next day, to $240.35.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $201.9B | $180.2B | +12% |
| AWS | $46.0B | $33.0B | +39% |
| North America | $112.9B | $106.3B | +6% |
| International | $43.1B | $40.9B | +5% |
| Advertising | $19.6B | $17.7B | +11% |
| Operating income | $28.1B | $17.4B | +61% |
| Net income | $20.7B | $21.2B | −2% |
| Earnings per share | $1.89 | $1.95 | −3% |

Net income fell even though operating income rose 61%. A year earlier Amazon booked $9.5 billion of pre-tax
gains on its investment in Anthropic; this quarter it had none of that size.

### Against Amazon's own July forecast

| | Forecast in July | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $197.0B to $202.0B | $201.9B |
| Operating income | $22.5B to $26.5B | $28.1B |

Revenue came in near the top of Amazon's range, and operating income $1.6 billion above the top of it.

### AWS speeds up, cash runs out

| Quarter | AWS growth | Free cash flow, past twelve months |
|---|---|---|
| Third quarter 2025 | +20% | $14.8B |
| Fourth quarter 2025 | +24% | $11.2B |
| First quarter 2026 | +28% | $1.2B |
| Second quarter 2026 | +37% | −$7.6B |
| Third quarter 2026 | +39% | −$42.4B |

AWS growth has sped up in every one of the last five quarters, and its operating margin was 38.7%. Over the same
five quarters, free cash flow, Amazon's own measure of cash from operations less spending on property and
equipment, went from a $14.8 billion inflow to a $42.4 billion outflow. Amazon puts it down to a $94.8 billion
rise in that spending, which it says "primarily reflects investments in artificial intelligence".

In the third quarter alone, cash from operations was $42.4 billion and spending on property and equipment, net
of sales and incentives, was $75.9 billion. By Amazon's own definition, that is an outflow of $33.5 billion in
three months.

### Where the money is going

| | 31 December 2025 | 30 September 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term debt | $65.6B | $173.1B |

Amazon expects to spend about $220 billion on capital this year. To pay for it, it has borrowed: long-term debt
rose $107.5 billion in nine months, and its 10-Q says: "We expect to undertake additional financing activities in
2026."

It is also investing in the AI companies it sells computing to. In the first nine months it put $50.0 billion
into OpenAI and $10.0 billion into Anthropic. At 30 September its holdings in private companies, mainly those two,
were carried at $143.7 billion.

### The orders behind the spending

Amazon's 10-Q puts the contracted revenue it has not yet earned, mostly at AWS, at about $578 billion, with an
average remaining life of 6.1 years. Two deals this year added about $100 billion each: OpenAI expanded its commitment to AWS by $100.0 billion
over 8.0 years, and Anthropic expanded its commitment by more than $100.0 billion over 10.0 years.

Amazon's chief executive, Andy Jassy, said in the release that AWS has "projected revenue growth rates of over
40% both in 2027 and in 2028".

### The outlook

| | Fourth-quarter forecast | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $240.0B to $247.0B | growth of 12% to 16% |
| Operating income | $28.5B to $33.5B | $25.0B |

Amazon expects advertising revenue to grow "in the high teens" in the fourth quarter, after 12% growth this
quarter, excluding currency moves, which it says was held back by the timing of Prime Day.

### How the shares reacted

Amazon closed at $243.79 on 29 October. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 30 October the
shares were at $240.35, down 1.4%. Of the 43 earlier results measured below, 36 were followed by a bigger move,
in either direction. Amazon is now 15.4% below its record close of $284.02, set on 3 August 2026.

### How Amazon shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 regular results releases before this one, from January 2016 to July 2026, comparing the close
on the day after Amazon reported, after the market closed, with the close on the day it reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **5.4%**.
- 24 of the 43 moved by 5% or more, and five by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **14.0%** in April 2022.
- The largest rise was **15.3%** in July 2026.
- The shares rose after 20 of the 43 and fell after 23.

### How far Amazon has fallen before

Amazon has fallen 40% or more five times since it listed in 1997. These are measured from a record close to the
lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 1998 | September 1998 | −47.7% | November 1998 |
| January 1999 | February 1999 | −51.5% | April 1999 |
| April 1999 | August 1999 | −59.3% | December 1999 |
| December 1999 | September 2001 | −94.4% | October 2009 |
| July 2021 | December 2022 | −56.1% | April 2024 |

All five got back to their record. The quickest, in 1999, was back within about two months of its low; the
slowest, after the 2001 low, took eight years. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **AWS.** Whether growth keeps rising towards the "over 40%" Jassy has projected for 2027.
- **Cash.** How much of this year's $220 billion of capital spending is paid for with new debt.
- **AI investments.** The value of the OpenAI and Anthropic holdings, which already swing Amazon's net income.
- **Advertising.** Whether growth returns to the high teens in the fourth quarter, as Amazon expects.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Amazon's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record of
what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account
your objectives, financial situation or needs.
