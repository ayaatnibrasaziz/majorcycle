## Body

### The short version

Netflix reported its results for the three months to 30 September after the US market closed on
20 October 2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $12.87 billion, up 12% on a year earlier, and within 0.1% of the $12.86 billion
  Netflix had forecast in July.
- **Profit:** operating income of $4.46 billion, 4.5% above the company's own forecast, giving an
  operating margin of 34.6% against the 33.2% it expected. Netflix says this was mainly the
  timing of expenses, and its full-year margin forecast is unchanged at 31.5%.
- **Earnings per share:** $0.87, against Netflix's forecast of $0.82 and $0.59 a year earlier.
- **The full-year outlook:** revenue of $51.1 billion, at the lower half of the $51.0–51.4 billion
  range it gave in July, with an unchanged operating margin of 31.5%.
- **What changed:** advertising revenue for 2026 is now expected to be about **$2.7 billion**,
  not about $3 billion, and free cash flow about **$12.0 billion**, not $12.5 billion.

All figures are in US dollars. Share prices and earnings per share are on the basis of Netflix's
ten-for-one share split of November 2025, as Netflix itself reports them.

### The quarter against Netflix's own forecast

Netflix publishes its own forecast for the next quarter with every set of results, and says it
is its "actual internal forecast", so the cleanest test of a quarter is against that number.

| | Netflix's forecast (July) | Reported | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.86B | $12.87B | $11.51B |
| Revenue growth | 11.7% | 11.8% | 17.2% |
| Operating income | $4.27B | $4.46B | $3.25B |
| Operating margin | 33.2% | 34.6% | 28.2% |
| Net income | $3.45B | $3.66B | $2.55B |
| Diluted earnings per share | $0.82 | $0.87 | $0.59 |

Growth is slowing. Revenue grew 17.2% in the third quarter of 2025, 16.2% in the first quarter of
2026, 13.4% in the second, and 11.8% now. Netflix forecasts 11.5% for the fourth quarter. That
slowdown was in Netflix's forecast and is not a surprise in itself.

By region, measured in US dollars, every part of the business grew by more than 10%:

| Region | Revenue | Growth | Growth without currency moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| US and Canada | $5.63B | 11% | 11% |
| Europe, Middle East and Africa | $4.11B | 11% | 9% |
| Latin America | $1.60B | 17% | 12% |
| Asia-Pacific | $1.54B | 12% | 15% |

Netflix no longer publishes its number of subscribers each quarter: it stopped with its
first-quarter 2025 results, and now announces only milestones. The last was 325 million paid memberships, passed in the
fourth quarter of 2025. Instead, it says that in the third quarter churn, the rate at which
members cancel, improved on a year earlier and was better than forecast, globally and in the US.

### What changed: the advertising forecast

The biggest change was to advertising. Netflix started selling ads in November 2022,
and its ad revenue grew more than two and a half times to over $1.5 billion in 2025. In January it said it expected ad revenue
to roughly double in 2026, and in April and July it put that at about $3 billion.

It now expects **about $2.7 billion**. That is around 10% below what it had said, though co-CEO Greg Peters said in the earnings
interview that the ads business is still growing by "over 80% this year". Netflix's explanation is a "slightly slower than expected rollout of some
ad product capabilities". It also said subscription revenue had done better than it expected.
Full-year revenue moved only slightly: $51.1 billion is 0.2% below the middle of its July range.

Netflix also confirmed that its cheaper plan with ads will launch in 15 more countries in 2027,
starting in March with nine in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, plus Colombia, Peru and
**New Zealand**. Australia has had an ads plan since November 2022.

In the interview, Peters said Netflix had set itself an "aggressive product road map" and was
"currently a bit behind our aggressive target for the year", because of "delays in the rollout
and partner integrations for some of those new ads products".

### The layoffs, and a new head of content

In the week before the results, Netflix made two big staff changes.

- **Job cuts.** On 14 October, Netflix told staff it would cut about 1,000 jobs, around 6% of its
  workforce, mostly in product, technology, legal and human resources, as reported by
  [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/media/netflix-layoffs-rcna603454) and
  [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/14/netflix-to-lay-off-1000-staff-amid-intensifying-competition-for-user-attention/),
  to which Netflix confirmed the cuts. The shareholder letter refers to the "recently announced
  headcount reductions" and gives their cost: about 1.2 percentage points off its fourth-quarter
  operating margin, which on its revenue forecast is roughly $160 million, and about 0.3 points
  off the full year.
- **A new chief content officer.** On 15 October Netflix announced that Peter Rice, formerly
  chairman of Walt Disney Television, will run its global content team from 26 October. He
  replaces Bela Bajaria, who is leaving after nearly 10 years
  ([Netflix](https://about.netflix.com/en/news/peter-rice-named-netflix-chief-content-officer)).

Netflix's fourth-quarter operating margin forecast is 26.0%, including the cost of the cuts,
against 24.5% a year earlier. The letter says the changes let it "continue to aggressively invest
in high impact areas".

Asked why, Peters said that after years of fast growth "there are some areas that we built
quickly and we ended up over invested in". CFO Spence Neumann said Netflix will not put
a number on the savings, and will give its 2027 forecasts, including its margin, in January.

### The question about viewing

The concern investors had been raising before the results was viewing time. In July Netflix
said view hours grew 2% in the first half of 2026, after 1.5% growth in 2025. This letter gives
no global figure for the third quarter. It addresses the concern directly instead:

> "We know there's some investor concern about our view hours growth. … But we do seek to
> accelerate growth in view hours as well."

Its evidence that members are still engaged is that its 2026 price rises have landed the way
past ones did. It gives India as an example, where view hours grew 24% on a year earlier and
revenue grew about 18% excluding currency moves.

The interview was more direct than the letter. Neumann: "we want to grow view hours faster.
We're not satisfied. We're working on it". And the weak spot is the US. Co-CEO
Ted Sarandos: "We do need better execution in the U.S. … It's just not growing as fast as it
could or as fast as we'd like". He described bringing in Peter Rice as a chance to take
Netflix's slate "to the next level, especially in the U.S.".

One remark matters for Australian readers. Asked where growth will come from, Peters named the
US, the UK and **Australia** as Netflix's "most mature markets", where it is "more deeply
penetrated", so its growth there is "going to skew towards" higher revenue per member and
advertising. In newer markets such as India, he said, more members will be the
biggest driver.

### Where the cash went

Netflix generated $2.9 billion of free cash flow in the quarter and spent $4.8 billion buying back
63.9 million of its own shares, its largest quarter of buybacks ever, at an average of about $75
a share. It has $22.3 billion left under its buyback authorisations. Its average diluted share
count was 3.3% lower than a year earlier.

It ended September with $8.2 billion of cash and $15.3 billion of debt. The $1 billion it borrowed
in the quarter is to repay a $1 billion bond falling due in the fourth quarter.

It now expects about $12.0 billion of free cash flow for 2026, down from $12.5 billion, which it
puts mainly down to a real estate transaction it expects to complete in the fourth quarter.

### How the shares reacted

On 21 October, the first full trading day after the results, Netflix closed at $65.04, down 5.3%
from $68.69 the day before. It was its lowest close since August 2024. At that close, the shares
were:

- **51.4% below their record close** of $133.91 on 30 June 2025,
- **44.3% below their highest price of the past year**, $116.73 on 13 November 2025,
- **30.6% lower than at the start of 2026.**

The US market has not fallen with it. On the same day the S&P 500 rose 0.6%, closing 0.8% below
its record close, set on 6 October.

### How Netflix shares usually react to results

Big moves the day after results are normal for Netflix. We measured all 44 regular results
releases from January 2016 to October 2026, comparing the close on the day after with the close on
the day Netflix reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **7.3%**. Since 2022 it was **8.8%**.
- 13 of the 44 moved by 10% or more. The largest fall was **35.1%** in April 2022, when Netflix
  reported a loss of 200,000 paid memberships against its own forecast of a gain of 2.5 million. The largest rise was **19.0%** in
  October 2016.
- The shares fell after 28 of the 44.
- **They have now fallen after each of the last six**: July 2025 (−5.1%), October 2025 (−10.1%),
  January 2026 (−2.2%), April 2026 (−9.7%), July 2026 (−7.3%) and October 2026 (−5.3%).

### How this fall compares with Netflix's past falls

Netflix has fallen this far before, several times. These are every fall of 40% or more, measured
from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 2002 | October 2002 | −70.8% | March 2003 |
| January 2004 | March 2005 | −76.7% | April 2008 |
| April 2008 | October 2008 | −55.9% | March 2009 |
| July 2011 | September 2012 | −82.0% | September 2013 |
| July 2018 | December 2018 | −44.2% | April 2020 |
| November 2021 | May 2022 | −75.9% | August 2024 |
| June 2025 | October 2026 (so far) | −51.4% | Not yet |

The current fall reached its deepest point so far on 21 October 2026, the day after the results,
51.4% below the record. It is the sixth-deepest of Netflix's seven falls of 40% or more since it listed in 2002. Each of the
earlier six got back to its record, taking between about five months and just over three years
from the low. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

Our stock page measures a fall from the highest price of the last 252 trading days, which on
21 October still included last year's results day, 21 October 2025, at $124.86. On that setting,
Netflix normally falls about 26.9% below that high before turning. On 21 October it was 47.9% below.

### What comes next

- **The fourth quarter.** Netflix forecasts revenue of $13.44 billion and earnings of $0.67 a
  share. Its slate includes three NFL games (Thanksgiving Eve and a Christmas Day doubleheader),
  the Tyson Fury vs Anthony Joshua fight, the final season of Emily in Paris, and the film
  The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.
- **The next results.** Netflix said it will report next in January, and Neumann said it will give
  its 2027 forecasts then. It has not set a date. Its fourth-quarter results came out on
  23 January 2024, 21 January 2025 and 20 January 2026.
- **Ads in 15 more countries,** from March 2027.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter and Netflix's own forecasts for the next. A forecast is a
company's estimate and can be wrong, as the advertising number shows. The share-price history is
a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does
not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
