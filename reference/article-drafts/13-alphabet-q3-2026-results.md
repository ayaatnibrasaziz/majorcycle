## Body

### The short version

Alphabet reported its results for the three months to 30 September after the US market closed on 28 October
2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $127.6 billion, up 25% from $102.3 billion a year earlier. Alphabet says it is now on a
  "half-trillion dollar revenue run rate".
- **Google Cloud:** revenue of $30.7 billion, up 103%, and operating income of $11.1 billion, up from
  $3.6 billion.
- **Profit:** operating income of $40.6 billion, up 30%. Net income was $26.5 billion, down 24%, because of
  an $8.7 billion loss on shares Alphabet holds, which its 10-Q says came largely from SpaceX.
- **Spending:** $51.4 billion of cash spent on property and equipment, against $24.0 billion a year earlier.
- **Free cash flow:** negative $9.1 billion, after negative $5.9 billion the quarter before.
- **Backlog:** $567.7 billion of signed revenue not yet recognised, $560.8 billion of it in Google Cloud.

All figures are in US dollars.

Costs grew more slowly than revenue: total costs and expenses rose 22%, with research and development up 36%.
Alphabet had 204,757 employees at the end of September, 8% more than a year earlier.

Alphabet says Search query growth was the highest it has been in the last five years, and that it passed 150
million YouTube Music and Premium subscribers, including trials. Google Cloud is now almost a quarter of
Alphabet's revenue, up from about 15% a year earlier.

### Google Cloud

Cloud's revenue doubled, and its profit rose faster. Operating income more than tripled, from $3.6 billion to
$11.1 billion, so its operating margin rose to 36.2%, from 23.7%.

Alphabet says sales of TPU systems, built on its own Tensor Processing Unit chips, "were also a meaningful
contributor to revenue growth". Its 10-Q describes these as hardware, software, installation and support sold to enterprise
customers. Chief executive Sundar Pichai said Alphabet's own AI models are processing "nearly 32 billion tokens
per minute, more than four times what we processed a year ago", and that the Gemini app "has reached one
billion users".

### Why profit fell: SpaceX

The fall in net income came from below the operating line. Alphabet's 10-Q shows it held $75.2 billion of
SpaceX shares at the end of September, subject to short-term limits on selling them. SpaceX went public this
year, so the value of those shares now moves with its share price, and every move runs through Alphabet's
profit.

A year earlier, gains on the shares Alphabet holds added $10.7 billion. This quarter they lost $8.7 billion,
and Alphabet says that loss cut its net income by $6.7 billion after tax. Its 10-Q says the swing in other
income came from "net unrealized losses in our marketable equity securities largely from SpaceX". Operating
income, which leaves investments out, rose 30%.

### Where the money is going

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $42.3B | $48.4B |
| Cash spent on property and equipment | $51.4B | $24.0B |
| Free cash flow | −$9.1B | $24.5B |
| Depreciation of property and equipment | $8.4B | $5.6B |

Alphabet defines free cash flow as cash from operations less capital spending. Spending rose 114%, and for the
second quarter in a row it was more than the business brought in. The depreciation charge, which spreads the
cost of that equipment over its life, rose 49%.

In July, Alphabet said it expected 2026 capital spending of $195 billion to $205 billion. It spent
$132.0 billion in the first nine months, which leaves $63 billion to $73 billion for the last three, more than
in any quarter so far. In July it also said spending would "increase significantly in 2027". And it warned that renting outside
computing capacity while it builds its own would "create modest margin pressure in the near-term".

Its 10-Q adds a commitment that is not yet on the balance sheet: leases, "primarily related to data centers and
energy infrastructure", with future payments of $130.5 billion that have not yet commenced. They start between
2026 and 2031.

### How Alphabet is paying for it

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| New debt raised | $29.0B | $6.6B |
| New shares sold | $12.4B | $0 |
| Shares bought back | $0 | $11.5B |

In the quarter, Alphabet issued senior unsecured notes for net proceeds of $28.7 billion and sold $12.4 billion
of new shares through its at-the-market program. That follows its June announcement of an $84.75 billion
equity raise to expand its AI infrastructure and computing capacity, which included a $10 billion investment by Berkshire
Hathaway. It still pays a dividend: $0.22 a share on its common stock, payable on 14 December.

### The backlog

At the end of September, Alphabet had $567.7 billion of revenue backlog, contracts signed but not yet
recognised, of which $560.8 billion was Google Cloud. It expects to recognise just over 55% of it over the next
24 months. In July, Google Cloud's backlog was $514 billion.

### Other Bets and Waymo

Other Bets, which earns its revenue primarily from autonomous transportation and internet services, brought in
$372 million and lost $2.0 billion, against a
$1.4 billion loss a year earlier. In October, Waymo took out a $5.0 billion senior secured loan maturing in
October 2032, at Term SOFR plus 5.25%. Alphabet's 10-Q says the loan is not guaranteed by Alphabet or any of its
other subsidiaries.

### What management said

Pichai: "We had a strong quarter and are now on a half-trillion dollar revenue run rate. Our deep full stack
approach is driving momentum across the business," with revenue up 25%.

### How the shares reacted

Alphabet's Class A shares closed at $324.89 on 28 October, before the results came out. By the close on 29 October they were at $283.82, down 12.6%. Of the 43 earlier results measured below, going back to February 2016, none was followed by a move that large in either direction: the largest fall was 9.5%, in October 2023, and the largest rise 10.2%, in April 2024.
