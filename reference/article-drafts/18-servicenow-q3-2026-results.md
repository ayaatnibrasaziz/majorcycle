## Body

### The short version

ServiceNow, which sells software that companies use to run their IT, customer service and other workflows,
reported results for the three months to 30 September 2026 after the US market closed on 28 October. In short:

- **Revenue:** subscription revenue of $3.99 billion, up 21% from $3.30 billion a year earlier, and $8 million
  above the top of the forecast it gave in July.
- **Profit:** net income of $277 million, down from $502 million a year earlier, as the cost of its acquisitions
  and borrowing came through.
- **AI:** it raised its year-end target for AI contracts for the second time this year, to $1.7 billion.
- **The forecast:** revenue under contract for the next 12 months to grow 18.5% in the fourth quarter, the lowest
  of its last five forecasts.
- **The shares:** down 26.9% the next day, to $109.67, the worst day since ServiceNow listed in 2012.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Three months to 30 September 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscription revenue | $3.99B | $3.30B | +21% |
| Total revenue | $4.11B | $3.41B | +20.5% |
| Operating income | $385M | $572M | −33% |
| Net income | $277M | $502M | −45% |
| Earnings per share, reported | $0.27 | $0.48 | |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $1.00 | $0.95 | |
| Free cash flow (ServiceNow's measure) | $822M | $592M | +39% |

ServiceNow's own adjusted measures leave out share-based pay, the amortisation of what it bought, deal costs and
severance. On those, its operating margin was 32.5%, against 33.5% a year earlier.

### Against ServiceNow's own forecast

| | Forecast in July | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription revenue | $3,975M to $3,980M | $3,988M |
| Revenue under contract for the next 12 months, growth | 20% | 21% |
| Operating margin, non-GAAP | 31% | 32.5% |

Growth in revenue under contract, which ServiceNow calls current remaining performance obligations, is measured
at steady exchange rates. Its finance chief, Gina Mastantuono, said "strong early renewals in Q3" contributed
"about half of the beat in Q3", and that "we pulled those deals out of Q4 and into Q3".

ServiceNow spent $9.2 billion on acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026. The largest was Armis, a security
company, bought in April for about $7.6 billion in cash; it also bought Veza, an identity security company, for
about $1.2 billion. To pay for Armis it borrowed $4.0 billion in April, then issued $4.0 billion of bonds in May.
Its debt is now $5.5 billion of bonds and $2.1 billion of commercial paper.

It bought back $2.2 billion of its own shares earlier in the year, but none in the third quarter.

### The forecast that moved the shares

| Quarter to | ServiceNow's forecast | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| December 2025 | 19% | 21% |
| March 2026 | 20% | 21% |
| June 2026 | 19.5% | 21.5% |
| September 2026 | 20% | 21% |
| December 2026 | 18.5% | |

These are its forecasts for growth in revenue under contract for the next 12 months, at steady exchange rates.
The new one is the lowest of the five. In each of the four quarters before it, ServiceNow beat its own forecast
by one or two points.

Mastantuono gave three reasons for the lower figure: the early renewals, "higher on-prem mix in Q4" from strong
public-sector demand, and its investment in helping large customers deploy AI. She said the on-premises deals
"contribute to revenue but not cRPO".

### The question on the call

Analysts asked when AI would lift growth across the whole business. One asked whether AI "is cannibalistic or
accretive to software companies". Chief executive Bill McDermott said the "true acceleration" would come in
2027.

ServiceNow raised its target for AI annual contract value to $1.7 billion by the end of 2026, from $1 billion at
the start of the year. Mastantuono said its own use of AI had crossed "$1 billion in efficiency gains", and
McDermott said "our ultimate headcount has actually slightly declined".

### How the shares reacted

ServiceNow closed at $150.13 on 28 October. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 29 October
the shares were at $109.67, down 26.9%, the worst day since it listed in June 2012. On 30 October they closed at
$105.12, 55.1% below their record close of $234.08 in January 2025 (adjusted for its five-for-one share split in
December 2025).

It was the fourth time in a row the shares fell after results: by 9.9% in January, 17.7% in April, 3.7% in July,
and now 26.9%.

### How ServiceNow shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 regular results releases before this one, from January 2016 to July 2026, comparing the close
on the day after ServiceNow reported, after the market closed, with the close on the day it reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **4.6%**.
- 20 of the 43 moved by 5% or more, and eight by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **17.7%** in April 2026.
- The largest rise was **15.5%** in April 2025.
- The shares rose after 32 of the 43 and fell after 11.

### How far ServiceNow has fallen before

ServiceNow has fallen 40% or more three times since it listed in 2012. These are measured from a record close to
the lowest close before the shares got back to it, adjusted for the share split:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 2015 | February 2016 | −47.6% | January 2017 |
| November 2021 | October 2022 | −51.3% | December 2023 |
| January 2025 | April 2026 | −64.5% | Not yet |

The first two got back to their record, about 12 and 14 months after their lows. The current fall is the deepest
of the three. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The backlog.** Whether revenue under contract grows by more than the 18.5% ServiceNow has forecast, as it has
  in each of the last four quarters.
- **AI contracts.** Whether they reach the $1.7 billion target by the end of the year.
- **The debt.** How fast the $7.6 billion is paid down, and whether buybacks resume.
- **2027.** Whether the acceleration its chief executive expects shows up in the numbers.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are ServiceNow's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record
of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account
your objectives, financial situation or needs.
