## Body

### The short version

Cisco, which makes the networking equipment that connects computers, offices and data centres, reported its results
for the three months to 24 October 2026, the first quarter of its financial year, after the market closed on
12 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $18,517 million, a record, up 24% from $14,883 million a year earlier.
- **Networking:** product revenue of $11,132 million, up 43%.
- **Profit:** earnings per share of $1.14, up 58%. On Cisco's own adjusted measure, $1.40, up 40%.
- **Orders:** product orders up 21%, slower than the 35% of the quarter before.
- **Margin:** gross margin down from 65.5% to 63.7%, which Cisco put down to selling more hardware and higher memory
  costs.
- **The forecast:** revenue for the year to July 2027 of $74.8 billion to $75.8 billion, up from $72.2 billion to
  $73.4 billion.
- **The shares:** down 6.2% on 13 November, to $95.64, while the S&P 500 fell 0.4%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $18,517M | $14,883M | +24% |
| Gross margin | 63.7% | 65.5% | −1.8 points |
| Operating income | $5,598M | $3,363M | +66% |
| Net income | $4,552M | $2,860M | +59% |
| Earnings per share | $1.14 | $0.72 | +58% |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $1.40 | $1.00 | +40% |

Chief executive Chuck Robbins said it was Cisco's "strongest revenue and non-GAAP earnings per share growth rates for
Cisco in over 15 years". Non-GAAP is Cisco's adjusted measure. It mainly leaves out the cost of the shares it pays its staff
in, $0.23 a share this quarter, and an accounting charge for the companies it has bought, $0.10.

### Where the revenue came from

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Networking | $11,132M | +43% |
| Security | $2,120M | +7% |
| Collaboration | $1,136M | +8% |
| Observability | $279M | +2% |
| All products | $14,667M | +32% |
| Services | $3,850M | +1% |

Almost all the growth came from networking. Revenue from the Americas rose 28%, from Europe, the Middle East and
Africa 18%, and from Asia Pacific, Japan and China 21%.

### The orders

Revenue is what Cisco delivered in the quarter. Orders are what customers asked for, so they say more about what is
coming. Product orders rose 21%, against 35% in the quarter to July:

| Orders, change on a year earlier | Aug–Oct 2026 | May–Jul 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| All product orders | +21% | +35% |
| Leaving out the largest cloud companies | +14% | +25% |
| Networking | +28% | +40% |

Asked about a fall in orders for artificial intelligence equipment from the largest cloud companies, which Cisco
calls hyperscalers, compared with the quarter before, Robbins said: "it's going to be lumpy, and there's going to
be some quarters that are going to be big, others that are going to be smaller."

### Why the margin fell

Cisco's gross margin, the share of revenue left after the cost of making what it sells, fell from 65.5% to 63.7%.
In its quarterly report, Cisco said product gross margin fell "primarily driven by a product mix shift toward
hardware and higher memory costs". Hardware earns Cisco less per dollar of revenue than software and services, and
the price of the memory chips that go into its equipment has risen.

Cisco expects the mix to keep moving that way. Finance chief Mark Patterson said AI equipment for the hyperscalers
would be 12% of Cisco's revenue in the year to July 2027, up from 6%. He said he was comfortable with analysts'
expectations of a full-year adjusted gross margin "just shy of the 65%".

### AI equipment for the cloud companies

In August, Cisco said it had delivered about $4 billion of AI equipment to the hyperscalers in the year to July 2026
and expected $7.5 billion in the year to July 2027. It now expects $9 billion, more than double the year before.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts had expected adjusted earnings per share of $1.33 and revenue of $18.12 billion, Trefis reported. Cisco
reported $1.40 and $18,517 million. Its own forecast in August had been revenue of $18.0 billion to $18.2 billion
and adjusted earnings per share of $1.32 to $1.34.

### The forecast

| | Forecast in August | Forecast now |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue, year to July 2027 | $72.2B to $73.4B | $74.8B to $75.8B |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $5.05 to $5.11 | $5.47 to $5.53 |

For the quarter to January 2027, Cisco expects revenue of $18.0 billion to $18.2 billion, slightly less than the
quarter just reported, and adjusted earnings per share of $1.34 to $1.36.

### Cash and returns

Cisco generated $4.4 billion of cash from operations in the quarter, up 36%. It returned $3.7 billion to
shareholders: $1.7 billion in dividends and $2.0 billion buying back about 18 million of its own shares, at an
average of $112.38. It declared a quarterly dividend of $0.42 a share, a rate of $1.68 a year, 1.8% of the share
price on 16 November.

### How the shares reacted

Cisco closed at $102.01 on 12 November. On 13 November it opened at $100.47, fell as low as $95.26, and closed at
$95.64, down 6.2%. The S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.9% the same day. Of the 15 other large companies
that make communications equipment, 14 fell too, Trefis reported, with a median fall of 4.5%.

A week before, on 5 November, the shares had fallen 7.6%, the day Morgan Stanley lowered its rating on Cisco, 24/7
Wall St. reported, and a day the S&P 500 fell 4.4%. On 16 November they closed at $94.52.

That leaves them 27.3% below their record close of $130.00 on 4 June 2026, though still up 22.7% since the end of
2025.

### How Cisco shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of Cisco's results releases before this one, from February 2016 to August 2026. Cisco reports
after the market closes, so we compared the close on the next day with the close on the day it reported.

- The middle move, up or down, was **4.8%**.
- 19 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and 4 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **13.7%** in May 2022.
- The largest rise was **13.4%** in May 2026.
- The shares rose after 24 of the 43 and fell after 19.
- The three results before this one moved the shares down **12.3%** in February, up **13.4%** in May and down
  **8.4%** in August 2026.

So a 6.2% fall is bigger than Cisco's usual move, though 13 of the 43 earlier results moved the shares more.

### How far Cisco has fallen before

Counting dividends, Cisco's shares have fallen 25% or more ten times since 1990, the start of our price history,
including this year. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |

| February 1991 | April 1991 | −26.1% | April 1991 |
| August 1993 | September 1993 | −28.9% | December 1993 |
| March 1994 | July 1994 | −51.4% | April 1995 |
| November 1995 | January 1996 | −25.6% | February 1996 |
| January 1997 | April 1997 | −38.1% | July 1997 |

| March 2000 | October 2002 | −89.3% | August 2021 |

This year's fall, to $93.85 on 6 November, is 27.8% in the share price alone and 27.3% counting dividends. The
nine earlier falls all got back to their record. The fall that began in March 2000 took more than 21 years, even
counting dividends. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The margin.** Whether the full-year adjusted gross margin lands near 65%, as Patterson expects, with memory
