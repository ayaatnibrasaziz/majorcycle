## Body

### The short version

AMD, which designs processors and AI chips for data centres, PCs and games consoles, reported results for the three
months to 26 September 2026 after the US market closed on 3 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** a record $13,377 million, up 45% from $9,246 million a year earlier, and above the top of the
  forecast it gave in August.
- **Data centre:** a record $8.5 billion, up 97%.
- **Profit:** net income of $2,428 million, up 95%, after a $1,097 million charge for warrants issued under a
  customer agreement.
- **The forecast:** revenue of about $17 billion in the fourth quarter, up about 66%, and more than 80% growth in
  2027.
- **Cash:** free cash flow of $554 million, down from $1,530 million a year earlier, as inventory rose by about
  $3 billion in the quarter.
- **The shares:** up 1.0% the next day, to $488.78.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Three months to Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $13,377M | $9,246M | +45% |
| Gross margin | 54% | 52% | +2 points |
| Operating income | $2,832M | $1,270M | +123% |
| Net income | $2,428M | $1,243M | +95% |
| Earnings per share, reported | $1.46 | $0.75 | +95% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $2.09 | $1.20 | +74% |
| Free cash flow (AMD's measure) | $554M | $1,530M | −64% |

AMD's own non-GAAP measures leave out share-based pay, the amortisation of what it bought, and the warrant charge.
On those, its gross margin was 56% and its operating margin 29%.

### Where the growth came from

| | Three months to Sep 2026 | Change on a year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Data Center | $8.5B | +97% |
| Client (PC processors) | $2.9B | +7% |
| Gaming | $773M | −40% |
| Embedded | $1.1B | +29% |

AMD said data centre sales came from its EPYC server processors and the launch of its Instinct MI450 AI chips.
Chief executive Lisa Su said both cloud and enterprise server sales more than doubled. Gaming fell on lower sales
of the custom chips it makes for games consoles, and of graphics cards.

### Against AMD's own forecast

| Quarter to | AMD's forecast | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| September 2026 | $12.7B to $13.3B | $13.4B |
| December 2026 | $16.5B to $17.5B | |

The new forecast is about 27% more revenue than the quarter just reported. AMD expects its data centre sales to
grow about 50% on a year earlier in the fourth quarter, and its PC and gaming sales to fall by more than 20%. It expects a gross margin of about 55% on its own non-GAAP measure, against 56% in the third quarter.

### 2027

On the call, Su said AMD now expects "revenue to grow more than 80% in 2027", which she said was "more than twice
the long-term financial model" it set out a year ago. She said data centre would be "more than 80% of our total
revenue in 2027", and that server revenue would double. She said customers had expanded their plans, and that AMD had "secured more supply to support that
growth". AMD also agreed to buy two companies in the quarter, World Labs and Taalas, and expects both deals to close
by the end of the year.

She also said "PC sell-through slowed late in the quarter as higher memory and component costs weighed on
demand", and that AMD is planning for PC shipments across the market to fall by a double-digit percentage in 2027.

### The fine print

Three numbers in the report move the other way.

| | 26 September 2026 | 27 June 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory | $11.5B | $8.5B |
| Total debt | $7.1B | $3.2B |
| Free cash flow, three months | $554M | $1,558M |

Finance chief Jean Hu said inventory rose "approximately $3 billion sequentially to $11.5 billion to fulfill our
strong data center demand". AMD's debt includes $4.75 billion of bonds it sold in August.

The warrant charge is an accounting cost: AMD issued warrants, rights to buy its shares, under an AI chip agreement
with a customer, and records changes in their value as its share price moves. Hu said these "quarterly
noncash fair value adjustments are expected to increase".

### How the shares reacted

AMD closed at $483.90 on 3 November. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 4 November the shares
were at $488.78, up 1.0%. During the day they had been as high as $514.61, up 6.3%.

In the three sessions before the results, the shares fell 19.3%, from $578.44 on 28 October to $466.84 on
2 November, including a 10.9% fall on 30 October, when chip stocks fell after Intel's forecast, as Gotrade reported. On 4 November they were 24.7% below their record close of $649.42
on 6 October 2026, and up 128% since the end of 2025.

### How AMD shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 regular results releases before this one, from January 2016 to August 2026. For releases
after the market closed, we compared the close on the next day with the close on the day AMD reported. One, in
October 2020, came out before the open, alongside AMD's bid for Xilinx, so we used that day's close.

- The middle move, up or down, was **7.0%**.
- 30 of the 43 moved by 5% or more, and 15 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **24.2%** in May 2017.
- The largest rise was **52.3%** in April 2016.
- The shares rose after 19 of the 43 and fell after 24.

Only two of the 43 moved less than this one.

### How far AMD has fallen before

AMD has fallen 50% or more six times since 1980, the start of our price history. These are measured from a record
close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 1980 | September 1981 | −66.5% | January 1983 |
| August 1984 | October 1990 | −90.9% | March 1997 |
| March 1997 | August 1998 | −72.6% | March 2000 |
| June 2000 | July 2015 | −96.6% | January 2020 |
| November 2021 | October 2022 | −65.4% | January 2024 |
| March 2024 | April 2025 | −63.0% | October 2025 |

All six got back to their record. The longest took more than 19 years from the record; measured from the low,
the longest took more than six years. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether fourth-quarter revenue reaches about $17 billion.
- **2027.** Whether growth of more than 80% shows up, and how much of it comes from the MI450.
- **Cash.** Whether free cash flow recovers as the inventory built up for those sales is shipped.
- **PCs.** How AMD's PC business holds up if the market falls by double digits, as it expects.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are AMD's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record of what
happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account your
objectives, financial situation or needs.
