## Body

### The short version

Tesla reported its results for the three months to 30 September after the US market closed on
21 October 2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $28.05 billion, against $28.10 billion a year earlier.
- **Profit:** operating income of $718 million, down 56% from $1.62 billion. Net income was
  $787 million, down 43%.
- **Earnings per share:** $0.22, or $0.48 on Tesla's adjusted measure, which leaves out
  share-based pay and some investment gains and losses.
- **Cash:** $1.53 billion of free cash flow, and $45.45 billion of cash and investments.
- **Cars:** 486,532 delivered, 2% fewer than a year earlier. Production rose 4%, to 464,391.
- **What's new:** a board committee to explore a deeper relationship with SpaceX, and a lower
  spending forecast for 2026.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

Tesla does not give a revenue forecast, so the cleanest test is against the same quarter a year
earlier.

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $28.05B | $28.10B | −0.2% |
| Car sales and leasing | $20.56B | $21.21B | −3.0% |
| Energy storage and solar | $2.88B | $3.42B | −15.6% |
| Services and other | $4.61B | $3.48B | +32.5% |
| Gross profit | $5.49B | $5.05B | +8.7% |
| Operating expenses | $4.78B | $3.43B | +39.2% |
| Operating income | $0.72B | $1.62B | −55.8% |
| Operating margin | 2.6% | 5.8% | |
| Diluted earnings per share | $0.22 | $0.39 | |

Tesla made more on each sale: its gross margin rose to 19.6%, from 18.0%. The profit went on
spending. Operating expenses rose 39%, which Tesla puts down to its AI work, which it calls Super
Intelligence, and other research projects, to share-based pay, including its CEO's 2025 pay award,
and to overheads. Research and development alone cost $2.76 billion, up from $1.63 billion, a rise
of 69%.

Energy storage revenue fell, which Tesla says was partly due to "tariff-related customer refunds".
It also says it has its "highest-ever order rate and backlog" for storage. Services and other grew
32.5%. Within car revenue, Tesla points to higher sales of Full Self-Driving: its active customers,
counting both one-off purchases and monthly subscriptions, reached 1.74 million, up from
1.04 million a year earlier.

### Where the money is going

Tesla's spending is rising quickly, but less quickly than it said in July.

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $6.26B | $6.24B |
| Capital expenditure | $4.73B | $2.25B |
| Free cash flow | $1.53B | $3.99B |

In its second-quarter report, Tesla said it expected capital expenditure of "in excess of
$25 billion in 2026". In this quarter's report the figure is "in excess of $20 billion in 2026". It has
spent $13.01 billion in the first nine months, so the new figure points to about $7 billion or
more in the last three.

Tesla says the spending is driven by its Super Intelligence projects: computing power and data
centres, new manufacturing and research production lines, and the fleet of cars and other assets
it runs itself. It plans to take its computing power for training its driving and robot software
to 400 megawatts by the end of the year, and to start commissioning equipment in 2027 at a chip
factory it is building in Austin.

### Robotaxi, Optimus and the Semi

- **Robotaxi.** Tesla started giving Robotaxi rides in Cybercabs in Austin on 3 September. Its
  Robotaxi service had driven nearly two million miles unsupervised by the end of September.
- **Optimus.** Production of its humanoid robot began on Tesla's high-volume line in Fremont.
- **Tesla Semi.** Volume production of its heavy truck began in Nevada.

### SpaceX

Tesla's quarterly report, filed the day after the results, says its board "has formed a special
committee … comprising disinterested directors to explore deepening Tesla's relationship with
SpaceX, which could be through certain arrangements including without limitation a business
combination". It adds that "there is no assurance that any such Transaction will be consummated",
and that one would need the committee's recommendation and, if agreed, shareholder approval.

Tesla already owns a small part of SpaceX: it invested $2.00 billion in SpaceX shares in March
2026, a stake of less than 1%. Elon Musk is the chief executive of both companies.

### For Australian readers

Tesla says it achieved "record quarterly deliveries in several markets, including Canada,
Australia, Japan and Taiwan". It does not give a figure for Australia.

### How the shares reacted

On 22 October, the first full day of trading after the results, Tesla closed at $374.58, down 3.1%
from $386.47 the day before. The wider market fell too: the S&P 500 lost 1.2% and the Nasdaq
Composite 2.6% that day.

It was the fourth results day in a row on which Tesla's shares fell. After the fall, Tesla was:

- **23.5% below its record close** of $489.88 on 16 December 2025,
- **24.9% below its highest price of the past year**, $498.83 on 22 December 2025,
- **16.7% lower than at the start of 2026.**

The current dip has already been deeper. From that December record, Tesla fell as far as $298.32
on 29 July 2026, 39.1% below.

The US market is still near its high: on 22 October the S&P 500 was 2.0% below its record close,
set on 6 October.

### How Tesla shares usually react to results

Tesla's results days are big events. We measured all 44 regular results releases from
February 2016 to October 2026, comparing the close on the day after with the close on the day Tesla
reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **6.0%**. Since 2022 it was **9.5%**.
- 11 of the 44 moved by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **14.5%** in July 2026. That quarter, free cash flow was negative
  $1.09 billion, the first negative quarter since early 2024.
- The largest rise was **21.9%** in October 2024.
- The shares fell after 26 of the 44. The last four: January 2026 (−3.5%), April 2026 (−3.6%),
  July 2026 (−14.5%) and October 2026 (−3.1%).

### How far Tesla has fallen before

Tesla has fallen 35% or more nine times since it listed in June 2010, counting the current fall.
These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 2010 | February 2011 | −38.5% | March 2012 |
| September 2013 | November 2013 | −37.7% | February 2014 |
| September 2014 | February 2016 | −49.8% | April 2017 |
| September 2017 | June 2019 | −53.5% | December 2019 |
| February 2020 | March 2020 | −60.6% | June 2020 |
| January 2021 | March 2021 | −36.2% | October 2021 |
| November 2021 | January 2023 | −73.6% | December 2024 |
| December 2024 | April 2025 | −53.8% | December 2025 |
| December 2025 | July 2026 (deepest so far) | −39.1% | Not yet |

The first eight all got back to their record. How long it took did not follow how far they fell:
the 60.6% fall of 2020 was back about three months after its low, while the 73.6% fall of
2021–23 took almost two years. At 39.1%, the current fall is, so far, smaller than five of the
eight before it.
That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

Measured on our stock page's setting, Tesla normally falls about 27.3% below its one-year high
before turning. On 22 October it was 24.9% below, inside its normal range.

### What comes next

- **Spending.** Tesla expects capital expenditure of more than $20 billion in 2026.
- **SpaceX.** The board committee's work has no stated end date.
- **Robotaxi.** Tesla says it expects to reduce wait times in Austin with more cars in the fleet.
- **The next results.** Tesla has not set a date. Its fourth-quarter results came out on
  29 January 2025 and 28 January 2026.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. The share-price history is a record of what happened before,
not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account your
objectives, financial situation or needs.
