## Body

### The index looks calm. Most of the market does not

As at 15 October 2026, the ASX 200 index sat **6.2% below its high of 6 August**. That is a
smaller dip than usual: measured the same way across its history, the index normally falls 12.8%
before it turns.

The companies inside it tell a different story. The middle company in the ASX 200 sat **22.1%
below its own one-year high**, and 109 of the 200 were down more than 20%. The index holds up
because it is weighted by size, and its largest companies have fallen far less — Commonwealth
Bank 14.4%, BHP 13.8%, CSL 13.5%.

Twelve of the fifteen technology companies are more than 20% below their high, and eight are
more than half below. No other sector is close.

### Down a lot is not the same as down a lot for them

A 50% fall means something different for a small chip developer than for an accounting-software
company. Some shares routinely halve and come back; others almost never move that far. So for
every company we also measured **how far it normally falls**: the average of every dip of more
than 5% in its own price history, each measured from its highest price of the year before.

Three things stand out.

**The deepest falls are not the most unusual ones.** Weebit Nano is down the most, 72.3%, but it
normally falls 47.1%. It is a small semiconductor developer whose shares have always moved
violently. The most unusual falls belong to steadier businesses — Netwealth, Xero, PEXA, NEXTDC,
Iress and WiseTech are each down more than twice their normal amount.

**One is past anything in its record.** Netwealth is 53.2% below its high, and the deepest fall
in its listed history before this one was 49.4%. PEXA (−60.4% against a record of −61.4%) and
NEXTDC (−47.2% against −48.8%) are within two points of theirs.

**It is not the whole sector.** Codan, Block and Data#3 sit well inside their normal range, and
Technology One and Megaport only just past it.

### Technology is the outlier this year

Across the whole ASX 200, 99 of the 200 companies are past their own normal fall, which shows how
broad this year's weakness has been. Being more than twice past it is rarer — 21 companies — and
six of those 21 are technology companies, from a sector of fifteen.

| Measure | Technology | Rest of the ASX 200 |
|---|---|---|
| Companies | 15 | 185 |
| Past their normal fall | 12 (80%) | 87 (47%) |
| More than twice their normal fall | 6 (40%) | 15 (8%) |
| Middle company, below its one-year high | −53.2% | −20.8% |
| Middle company, normal fall | −25.2% | −21.4% |

The last row is the one worth sitting with. Technology companies do not normally fall much
further than the rest of the market, 25.2% against 21.4%. This year the middle one is down
53.2%, two and a half times the rest of the market's 20.8%.

### Technology falls have usually come back faster

For the history, we used the same method as
[How long does an ASX share take to recover?](/articles/how-long-does-an-asx-share-take-to-recover)
The clock starts when a share is 20% below its high and stops only when it closes back at that
high. We ran it again on these fifteen companies and on the whole index, to the same date as
everything above.

| Every 20% fall since 2000 | Technology | Whole ASX 200 |
|---|---|---|
| Falls measured | 104 | 1,265 |
| Typical wait to get back | 9 months | 11 months |
| Back within 1 year | 52.6% | 51.9% |
| Back within 3 years | 81.0% | 75.7% |
| Back within 5 years | 91.8% | 84.3% |
| Not back yet | 13 | 159 |

On this record, a 20% fall in an ASX technology share has come back a little faster and a
little more often than one in the rest of the market. It is the only good news in this article,
and the next two sections explain why it should be held loosely.

Two things in this table matter more than the waits.

**Eight of the fifteen are already deeper than any fall they have come back from.** WiseTech's
worst recovered fall was 72.7% and it has now been 79.2% down. Netwealth's was 44.9% against
58.1% now, NEXTDC's 48.4% against 52.0%, Iress's 58.1% against 64.7%, and Block's 34.3% against
68.8%. Xero's current fall has gone just past the 71.1% fall it came back from in 2018, to 71.5%.
Silex has been down 98.8% in a fall that began in 2007, and PEXA has not come back from the only
20% fall it has had since listing in 2021. For these eight, the company's own history has no
precedent for a recovery from here.

**Some of these falls are already old.** Silex has waited 19.3 years and is still 73.0% below
its 2007 record. Iress has waited 5.1 years, PEXA 4.6 and Block 4.5. Each is longer than any
wait those companies have come through before.

### The deeper the fall, the longer the wait

| How far it fell | Technology: typical wait | Technology: back within 5 years | Whole ASX 200: typical wait | Whole ASX 200: back within 5 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20% to 30% | 3 months | 100% | 3 months | 100% |
| 30% to 40% | 6 months | 100% | 8 months | 99% |
| 40% to 50% | 8 months | 100% | 1.4 years | 94% |
| 50% to 70% | 2.8 years | 89% | 3.0 years | 72% |
| More than 70% | 4.9 years | 58% | 7.7 years | 33% |

"Back within 5 years" counts only falls with five full years of history behind them. For
technology that is a small number in the bottom two rows — 9 falls of 50% to 70%, and 12 past
70% — so those figures could move a long way on one or two companies.

The thirteen technology companies in a fall today sit mostly in those bottom two rows. Measured
from each company's record high, eight have been between 50% and 70% down at their worst, and
four — Silex, Weebit Nano, WiseTech and Xero — more than 70%. Across the whole market, about two
in three falls past 70% were still not back five years later.

### What this cannot tell you

These are the fifteen technology companies in the ASX 200 **today**. Technology companies that
fell and were dropped from the index are not here, and they did worse than the ones that stayed.
That flatters the recovery figures above, probably more for technology than for the market as a
whole, because a company that falls far enough leaves the index. Read them as "what the history
of today's large Australian technology companies looks like", not as the odds facing any
particular share.

It is also a description of the past, and the past has no obligation to repeat. A deep fall is
where the better opportunities have historically been found, and it is also where the permanent
losses are. This cannot tell you which of the two any particular company is. Only the business
can.

### How to read the numbers

Two different highs are used here, on purpose. The first tables measure each company against its
**one-year high**, the highest price traded in the past 252 trading days, which is the "current
drawdown" on every stock page. The recovery tables measure from the **record high**, the highest
close since 2000 or since listing, because a fall has only recovered when the price is back above
the last peak. That is why Xero is 63.3% below its one-year high and 70.3% below its record.

Eight of the fifteen set their one-year high in October or November 2025, so those highs are
about to drop out of the one-year window. When they do, each will be measured from a lower high
and its fall will look smaller, without the share price moving. That is why this article is dated
and will not be updated in place.

"Technology" is each company's standard sector classification, not our judgement. It includes
Netwealth, PEXA and Iress, which some would call financial or property services, and two US-based
companies listed in Australia, Block and Life360. Weebit Nano listed in August 2016 through
Radar Iron, an iron-ore company, so its figures start on 3 August 2016, its first day of trading
as Weebit. This is the one place our figures differ from its stock page, which still includes the
older history.

Prices include dividends. Nothing here is advice about any company.
