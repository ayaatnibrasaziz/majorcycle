## Body

### The short version

Sandisk, which makes flash memory and the drives built on it, reported results for its first quarter of fiscal
2027, the three months to 2 October 2026, after the US market closed on 29 October. In short:

- **Revenue:** $11.0 billion, up 378% from $2.3 billion a year earlier, and up 23% from $9.0 billion in the
  quarter before.
- **Profit:** a gross margin of 85.0%, against 29.8% a year earlier, and net income of $8.1 billion, against
  $112 million.
- **Data centres:** $4.8 billion of revenue, up 61% in three months, from $269 million a year earlier.
- **The forecast:** second-quarter revenue of $12.2 billion to $12.6 billion.
- **The split:** eight shares for every one held, with split-adjusted trading expected to begin on 20 November.
- **The shares:** down 13.5% the next day, to $1,150.89, still 50.7% below the record close of $2,335.00
  set in June.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Three months to 2 October 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $11.0B | $2.3B | +378% |
| Gross margin | 85.0% | 29.8% | +55.2 points |
| Operating income | $8.8B | $0.2B | |
| Net income | $8.1B | $0.1B | |
| Earnings per share, reported | $52.60 | $0.75 | |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $49.51 | $1.22 | |

Sandisk's finance chief, Luis Visoso, said about 60% of the rise in revenue from the quarter before came from
selling more and about 40% from higher prices.

### Against Sandisk's own forecast

| | Forecast in August | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $10.3B to $10.8B | $11.0B |
| Gross margin, non-GAAP | 83% to 85% | 85.1% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $44 to $46 | $49.51 |

All three came in above the top of the range Sandisk gave in August.

### Five quarters of the memory shortage

| Quarter to | Revenue | Gross margin | Data centre |
|---|---|---|---|
| October 2025 | $2.3B | 29.8% | $0.3B |
| January 2026 | $3.0B | 50.9% | $0.4B |
| April 2026 | $6.0B | 78.4% | $1.5B |
| July 2026 | $9.0B | 84.6% | $3.0B |
| October 2026 | $11.0B | 85.0% | $4.8B |

In five quarters, quarterly revenue rose nearly fivefold and data-centre revenue nearly eighteenfold. Sandisk's
chief executive, David Goeckeler, said data centres have grown from "approximately 20% of industry bits early in
the decade to roughly half of the industry bits today", as AI systems store more of what they work on in flash
memory.

### Who paid more, and who bought less

Not every customer grew. Sandisk's revenue split three ways:

| | Three months to 2 October 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data centre | $4.8B | $0.3B | |
| Edge (phones, PCs, cars) | $5.8B | $1.4B | +320% |
| Consumer | $0.4B | $0.7B | −39% |

Consumer sales, its memory cards and drives sold in shops, were $397 million, down 29% from the quarter before,
"as the market adjusts to higher pricing", Visoso said. Within Edge, he said growth was "partially offset by
contraction at the lower end".

The higher memory prices that lifted Sandisk's revenue are the same ones Intel, reporting the same day, blamed for
weaker demand for PCs.

### Selling ahead

Sandisk now sells much of its future output under long-term agreements it calls "new business models". Visoso
said about 50% of its bits are under these agreements for 2027 and about two-thirds for 2028, and: "Our NBM
pipeline exceeds our supply throughout the end of the decade."

For the coming quarter, Sandisk's forecast "includes modest like-for-like price increases", and Visoso said he
expects "pricing to continue to increase modestly throughout the year".

### Where the money is going

| | Three months to 2 October 2026 |
|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $6.4B |
| Adjusted free cash flow (Sandisk's measure) | $5.7B |
| Shares bought back | $5.0B |
| Cash at the end of the quarter | $5.1B |

Sandisk spent $5.0 billion buying back about 3.2 million of its own shares in the quarter, and says it is
returning "100% of excess cash to shareholders". It has no debt. Its spending on equipment is small for a chip
company: $378 million in the quarter, 3.4% of revenue, which Visoso expects to rise to a mid-single-digit
percentage of revenue for the year.

### The outlook

| | Second-quarter forecast | First quarter |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $12.2B to $12.6B | $11.0B |
| Gross margin, non-GAAP | 84.5% to 85.5% | 85.1% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $55.00 to $57.00 | $49.51 |

After the 8-for-1 split, the forecast earnings per share would be $6.88 to $7.13.

### How the shares reacted

Sandisk closed at $1,330.05 on 29 October. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 30 October
the shares were at $1,150.89, down 13.5%. Of its six earlier results, only one was followed by a bigger move.

### How Sandisk shares usually react to results

Sandisk has reported six times before this since it became a separate company in February 2025. Comparing the
close on the day after it reported with the close on the day it reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **6.8%**.
- Four of the six moved by 5% or more, and one by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **6.8%** in August 2026.
- The largest rise was **15.3%** in November 2025.
- The shares rose after four of the six and fell after two.

### How far Sandisk has fallen before

Sandisk has fallen 20% or more five times since it listed in February 2025, counting the current fall. These are
measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 2025 | April 2025 | −47.5% | September 2025 |
| November 2025 | December 2025 | −31.3% | January 2026 |
| February 2026 | March 2026 | −24.2% | March 2026 |
| March 2026 | March 2026 | −25.9% | April 2026 |
| June 2026 | July 2026 | −56.5% | Not yet |

The first four got back to their record, the slowest about four months after its low. The current fall began at
a record close of $2,335.00 on 25 June 2026; at its lowest, on 29 July, the shares were 56.5% below it. That is a
record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **Prices.** Whether memory prices keep rising "modestly", as Sandisk expects, or start to fall.
- **Data centres.** Whether data-centre demand grows about 40% in 2027, as Visoso expects, after more than
  doubling in 2026.
- **Consumers and PCs.** Whether higher prices keep shrinking the cheaper end of the market.
- **The split.** Split-adjusted trading is expected to begin on 20 November.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Sandisk's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record of
what happened before, not a prediction, and Sandisk has been a separate company only since February 2025. This
article is general information and does not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
