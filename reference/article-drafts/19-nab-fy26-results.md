## Body

### The short version

National Australia Bank, one of Australia's four big banks and the one whose largest division serves business
customers, reported its results for the year to
30 September 2026 before the market opened on 5 November. In short:

- **Profit:** cash earnings, the measure NAB uses for its own performance, of $6,141 million, down 13.4% from
  $7,091 million.
- **The write-down:** most of that fall is a $1,347 million charge, $949 million after tax, from changing how it
  accounts for software. Without it, cash earnings were $7,090 million, flat on a year earlier.
- **Bad debts:** a credit impairment charge of $1,547 million, up from $833 million.
- **The dividend:** a final dividend of 85 cents, fully franked, the same as a year ago, for 170 cents for the
  year.
- **The shares:** down 5.4% on the day, to $38.96, while the S&P/ASX 200 rose 2.6%.

All figures are in Australian dollars.

### The year against a year earlier

| | Year to 30 September 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $18,512M | $17,398M | +6.4% |
| Net operating income | $22,014M | $20,813M | +5.8% |
| Operating expenses, without the write-down | $10,292M | $9,848M | +4.5% |
| Credit impairment charge | $1,547M | $833M | +85.7% |
| Cash earnings | $6,141M | $7,091M | −13.4% |
| Cash earnings, without the write-down | $7,090M | $7,091M | Flat |
| Statutory net profit | $6,143M | $6,759M | −9.1% |
| Dividend per share | 170c | 170c | Flat |

NAB's net interest margin, what it earns on its loans less what it pays for its funding, was 1.80% for the year,
up 0.06 of a percentage point. Without the write-down, its underlying profit, before bad debts and tax, rose 6.9%.

### The write-down, and why it is not the whole story

In the first half of the year NAB changed how it accounts for the software it builds. It shortened the life it
gives that software, and raised the cost at which a project is treated as an asset from $5 million to $20 million,
which NAB says brings it in line with its peers. That produced the $1,347 million charge. NAB says it has no
effect on its capital ratio, because software is already deducted from its capital.

So the 13.4% fall is mostly an accounting change. Without it, profit was flat. The part that is not accounting is
the next line.

### The bad debts

| Year to September | Credit impairment charge |
|---|---|
| 2022 | $125M |
| 2023 | $802M |
| 2024 | $728M |
| 2025 | $833M |
| 2026 | $1,547M |

Of this year's charge, $1,083 million was for individual customers, mainly businesses, and $464 million was
general provisions, including $403 million set aside because NAB expects a harder economy. In the second half,
NAB said more business customers were "experiencing early signs of stress", and that falling house prices had
hit its home-loan book.

Before the results, analysts had expected a charge of about $1,327 million and cash earnings of about
$6,382 million, according to The Bull. NAB reported $1,547 million and $6,141 million.

### The second half on its own

NAB reports twice a year. The second half, April to September, was weaker than the first on the measures that
leave out the write-down:

| | Six months to Sep 2026 | Six months to Mar 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash earnings, without the write-down | $3,502M | $3,588M | −2.4% |
| Credit impairment charge | $841M | $706M | +19.1% |
| Net interest margin | 1.79% | 1.81% | −0.02 points |

### Its business lending

Business customers are where most of NAB's growth, and most of its bad debts, came from. Its Business and Private Banking division earned $3,685 million, up 10.7%, and NAB's Australian
business lending grew 9.8% over the year. Its corporate and institutional bank earned $1,740 million, down 6.1%,
and its New Zealand bank earned NZ$1,381 million, down 7.0% in New Zealand dollars, both mainly on higher
bad-debt charges. (These divisional figures leave out the software write-down, which NAB books centrally.)

### The job cuts and next year

On 20 October, two weeks before the results, NAB said it would cut up to 2,500 roles, about 6% of its workforce,
in the year to September 2027, with the focus on its corporate functions, and expects a restructuring charge of about $300 million.

For the year ahead, NAB expects its underlying costs to grow by less than 3%, before that charge and the software
change. Its economists expect Australia's economy to grow 1.4% in 2026 and 1.0% in 2027, and the unemployment rate
to rise to about 5.5% by early 2028. Chief executive Andrew Irvine said he did not see a recession as the most
likely outcome, but that its chance had "probably increased".

### Its capital

NAB's common equity tier 1 ratio, the main measure of a bank's capital cushion, was 11.87% at 30 September,
against a regulatory minimum of 10.25% and NAB's own target of more than 11.25%. It will issue new shares at a
1.5% discount to shareholders who take this dividend in shares, expected to raise about $0.9 billion and lift the
ratio to about 12.07%.

### How the shares reacted

NAB closed at $41.17 on 4 November. It released its results before the market opened on 5 November, and the
shares closed that day at $38.96, down 5.4%, having been as low as $37.91 during the day. The S&P/ASX 200 rose
2.6% the same day. Of the other big banks, Commonwealth Bank closed flat and ANZ fell 0.6%. Westpac fell 1.0%,
but 5 November was its ex-dividend day: from then, buyers no longer received its 77-cent final dividend.

That leaves NAB 20.7% below its record close of $49.10 on 25 February 2026.

### How NAB shares usually react to results

We measured all 20 half-year and full-year results NAB released from May 2016 to May 2026, comparing the close on
the day of each release with the close the day before. We left out April 2020, when NAB's shares were in a
trading halt all day for a capital raising announced with its results.

- The middle move, up or down, was **1.2%**.
- Only one of the 20 moved by 5% or more: May 2023.
- The largest fall was **6.4%** in May 2023.
- The largest rise was **3.3%** in November 2020.
- The shares rose on 8 of the 20 days and fell on 12.

So this was NAB's second-largest results-day fall in more than ten years.

### How far NAB has fallen before

NAB's shares have fallen 30% or more four times since 1988, the start of our price history. These are measured
from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it, counting dividends:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| April 1999 | February 2000 | −30.8% | November 2000 |
| November 2007 | March 2009 | −60.7% | March 2013 |
| April 2015 | February 2016 | −31.6% | March 2017 |
| September 2019 | March 2020 | −52.2% | August 2021 |

All four got back to their record, the longest four years after its low. Counting dividends, the shares are now 18.9%
below their February 2026 record. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **Bad debts.** Whether the charge keeps rising in the first half of next year, especially in business lending.
- **Costs.** Whether underlying costs grow by less than the 3% NAB expects, as it cuts up to 2,500 roles.
- **The margin.** Whether the second half's small fall in its net interest margin continues.
- **The dividend.** The final dividend of 85 cents goes ex-dividend on 10 November and is paid on
  15 December 2026.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one year. Forecasts are NAB's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours. The share-price
history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
