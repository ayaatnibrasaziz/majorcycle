## Body

### The short version

Life360, the family location-sharing app company, reported its results for the three months to 30 September 2026
on 9 November in the US, which was the morning of 10 November in Sydney, before the ASX opened. All of its figures
are in US dollars. In short:

- **Revenue:** US$174.1 million, up 40%, a little faster than the 38% of the quarter before.
- **Monthly active users:** 107.7 million, up 17%, after adding 5.3 million in the quarter.
- **Paying Circles:** 3.4 million, up 26%, after a record 188,000 net additions for a third quarter.
- **Net income:** US$22.5 million, up 130%, including a US$17.5 million gain on the value of investments.
- **The outlook:** revenue for 2026 now expected to be US$665 million to US$681 million, from US$650 million to
  US$685 million; advertising revenue US$98 million to US$102 million, from US$98 million to US$115 million.
- **The shares:** down 15.3% on results day on the ASX, to A$18.91, and up 10.0% the next day.

### What Life360 reported

| Three months to 30 September, US$ | 2025 | 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Subscription revenue | 96.3M | 124.2M | +29% |
| Advertising revenue | 7.3M | 25.4M | +250% |
| Hardware revenue | 11.3M | 9.0M | −20% |
| Other revenue | 9.6M | 15.5M | +61% |
| **Total revenue** | **124.5M** | **174.1M** | **+40%** |

- **Subscriptions** are still most of the business. A Paying Circle is Life360's name for a group of members with a
  paid subscription, usually a family. Paying Circles grew 26% and the average revenue from each rose 4%, which
  Life360 puts down to more people choosing higher-priced plans and the early effect of price rises in the US.
- **Advertising** more than tripled, mostly because Life360 bought Nativo, an advertising technology company, for
  US$104.0 million in cash and shares. The deal was agreed in November 2025 and completed on 2 January 2026, so
  Nativo was not in last year's figures.
- **Hardware** fell, mainly because Life360 stopped selling its tracking devices through bricks-and-mortar stores.
- **Users:** Life360 had 55.8 million monthly active users in the US and 51.9 million outside it, the first time
  its international users have passed 50 million.

### Profit and cash

Net income was US$22.5 million, up from US$9.8 million a year earlier. US$17.5 million of it was a net gain on the
fair value of investments Life360 holds, which is not cash from running the business.

On its own adjusted measure, adjusted EBITDA, which leaves out items including share-based pay, the investment
gain, interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, Life360 earned US$34.6 million, up 41%, a margin of 20%.

Cash from operations rose 3% to US$27.2 million, below adjusted EBITDA, which Life360 says was mainly the timing of
receipts and payables. It ended the quarter with US$465.0 million of cash and short-term investments.

Gross margin slipped from 78% to 77%, because advertising earns a thinner margin than subscriptions. Operating
expenses rose 38%, a little slower than revenue, so they fell from 73% of revenue to 72%.

Life360 has also been buying back its own shares. Since it announced a US$225 million program in May 2026, it has
bought back US$44.4 million worth, leaving US$180.6 million.

### Why the shares fell

Profit beat analysts' forecasts: adjusted earnings per share of 38 US cents against about 24 cents expected, IBTimes
reported. But two things in the outlook weighed on the shares.

**The advertising forecast came down.** Life360 cut the top of its 2026 advertising forecast:

| Forecast for 2026, US$ | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | 650M to 685M | 665M to 681M |
| Subscription revenue | 475M to 480M | 483M to 488M |
| Advertising revenue | 98M to 115M | 98M to 102M |
| Adjusted EBITDA | 130M to 140M | 132M to 138M |

Total revenue and subscriptions moved up, and the middle of the adjusted EBITDA range stayed at US$135 million. But
advertising is the newer business, and the top of its range fell from US$115 million to US$102 million. On the
results call, as MarketBeat reported it, chief executive Lauren Antonoff said Life360 had paused programmatic
advertising for a period in the quarter while it improved ad quality, and that by the time it combined its
advertising sales teams it was late to win some advertisers' 2026 budgets. She said Life360 expects stronger
advertising growth in 2027.

**A slower three months to December.** Life360 has raised its prices in the US. On the same call, as MarketBeat
reported it, finance chief Russell Burke said US Paying Circle net additions in the fourth quarter were likely to be
about half what they otherwise would have been, largely because of a one-time rise in cancellations after the price
rise, and that Life360 expects growth to return to normal in the first quarter of 2027. Antonoff also said user
growth was heading towards the lower end of Life360's forecast of 17% to 20% for the year.

Two brokers turned more cautious: Bank of America and Macquarie each cut their rating on the shares to neutral,
IBTimes reported. Other analysts said the fall was overdone.

These are the company's and the brokers' views, not ours.

### How the shares reacted

Life360's ASX shares closed at A$22.33 on 9 November. On 10 November, results day, they closed at A$18.91, down
15.3%. The ASX 200 rose 1.1% the same day. On 11 November they rose 10.0% to A$20.81, and they closed at A$21.26 on
13 November.

Life360's ASX shares are CHESS Depositary Interests, three to each of its Nasdaq-listed shares.

### How Life360 shares usually react to results

We measured all 23 of Life360's results from August 2019, its first after it listed on the ASX in May 2019, to
August 2026, comparing the close on each results day with the close the day before. Life360 lodges its results
with the ASX before the market opens. Until February 2022 it reported every six months; since August 2022 it has
reported every quarter.

- The middle move, up or down, was **7.8%**.
- Nineteen of the 23 moved the shares 5% or more, and ten 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **28.8%** in February 2022.
- The largest rise was **38.5%** in March 2024.
- The shares rose on 15 of the 23 days and fell on 8.

So a 15.3% fall was a big move even for Life360: six of the 23 earlier results moved the shares more. Counting
this one, it was its fourth-largest results-day fall.

### How far Life360 has fallen

Life360's record close on the ASX was A$55.44 on 3 October 2025. From there the shares fell 67.7% to A$17.91 on
13 April 2026, their lowest close since. On 13 November they were 18.7% above that low, but still 61.7% below the
record, and down 36.6% since the start of 2026.

Life360's shares have fallen 15% or more six times since they listed on the ASX in May 2019. Life360 does not pay a
dividend, so these are price-only:

| From the close of | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the high |
|---|---|---|---|
| May 2019 (its first day) | March 2020 | −69.9% | April 2021 |
| September 2021 | October 2021 | −15.4% | October 2021 |

The five earlier falls all got back to where they started, the slowest about 22 months after its low. The deepest,
82.5%, took from November 2021 to April 2024 to recover. This is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.
