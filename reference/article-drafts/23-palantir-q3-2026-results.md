## The answer, shown under the heading

Palantir's revenue rose 96% to $2.31 billion in the third quarter of 2026, above analysts' forecasts, and it raised
its forecast for the year. But the value of the new contracts it signed grew just 6%, and its shares fell 1.6% the
next day, the smallest move after any of its results since it listed.

## Body

### The short version

Palantir, which sells software that organisations use to bring their data together and run artificial
intelligence on it, reported its results for the three months to 30 September 2026 after the market closed on
2 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $2,313 million, up 96% from $1,181 million a year earlier, and its first quarter above
  $2 billion.
- **US commercial:** revenue from American companies of $984 million, up 148%, more than it earned from the
  American government, $953 million, for the first time.
- **Profit:** net income of $1,128 million, up from $476 million.
- **New contracts:** total contract value, the lifetime value of the contracts it signed in the quarter, of
  $2,918 million, up 6%.
- **Palantir's explanation:** it said several of its largest new deals were "shorter in duration than usual",
  and that it expects contracts to grow longer and larger over time.
- **The forecast:** revenue for 2026 of $8,530 million to $8,534 million, up from the $8,150 million to
  $8,158 million it forecast in August.
- **The shares:** down 1.6% on 3 November, to $175.40, while the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.8%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,313M | $1,181M | +96% |
| Income from operations | $1,085M | $393M | +176% |
| Net income | $1,128M | $476M | +137% |

On its own accounting, that is an operating margin of 47% and a net margin of 49%. Palantir's own adjusted
measure, which mainly leaves out the cost of the shares it pays its staff in, put operating income at
$1,427 million, a 62% margin.

Net income was $144 million in the same quarter of 2024, so in two years it has grown nearly eightfold.

### Where the revenue came from

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | On a year earlier | On the quarter before |
|---|---|---|---|
| US commercial | $984M | +148% | +29% |
| US government | $953M | +96% | +18% |
| All US revenue | $1,937M | +119% | +23% |
| All revenue | $2,313M | +96% | +20% |

So 84% of Palantir's revenue came from the United States. Chief executive Alex Karp said: "Our U.S. commercial
revenue has surpassed our U.S. government revenue for the first time in our company's history."

### Its customers

Palantir closed 212 deals worth at least $1 million in the quarter, including 107 worth at least $5 million and
64 worth at least $10 million. On the results call, its chief financial officer, Dave Glazer, said:

- it had 686 US commercial customers, up 29% on a year earlier;
- its 20 largest customers each spent an average of $146 million with it over the past 12 months, up 76%;
- its net dollar retention, its measure of how much more existing customers spend than a year earlier, was 165%,
  up 8 points on the quarter before.

Its chief revenue officer, Ryan Taylor, gave examples of trials turning into much larger contracts within months.
One industrial materials company "piloted a nearly $3 million engagement in Q2 2026 before converting to a
$29 million ACV deal last quarter". ACV, annual contract value, is a contract's value per year.

### Against what analysts expected

Before the results, analysts' average forecast was revenue of $2.18 billion and adjusted earnings per share of
$0.41, according to XTB. Palantir reported $2,313 million and $0.44. In August, Palantir itself had forecast
third-quarter revenue of $2,160 million to $2,164 million.

For the fourth quarter, Palantir forecast revenue of $2,649 million to $2,653 million. Analysts had expected about
$2.46 billion, according to XTB.

### The new contracts

Revenue is what Palantir earned in the quarter. Its contract figures say what it signed, and they slowed sharply:

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | Apr–Jun 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Contract value signed | $2,918M | $3,373M |
| Change on a year earlier | +6% | +49% |
| Signed with US companies | $1,463M | $2,132M |
| Change on a year earlier | +11% | +153% |

Its remaining performance obligations, the revenue it is owed under contracts that customers cannot cancel, were
$4.9 billion at 30 September, the same as at 30 June. A year earlier they were $2.6 billion.

### What Palantir said about its contracts

On the results call, Glazer gave Palantir's explanation: "We signed multiple 8-figure deals in the quarter that
were shorter in duration than usual, as customers rapidly adopted initial Sovereign AI capabilities in their
enterprise." He added: "over time, we expect both the duration and size of these contracts to increase."

Total contract value counts a contract's whole life, so a one-year contract adds less to it than a three-year
contract worth the same each year.

Some of Palantir's other contract figures grew faster:

- Over the past 12 months, the contracts it signed with US companies had a total value of $6,114 million, up 62%
  on the 12 months before.
- Its remaining deal value, the total still to come from all its contracts, was $13.4 billion, up 55% on a year
  earlier but only 2% on the quarter before.
- Its remaining performance obligations were up 88% on a year earlier. Glazer said the measure "does not take
  into account contracts with an initial term of less than 12 months and contractual obligations that fall beyond
  termination for convenience clauses, both of which are common in most of our government business".

### Profit, cash and the full-year forecast

Palantir generated $1,216 million of adjusted free cash flow in the quarter, 53% of its revenue, and ended September
with $10.4 billion of cash and US Treasury securities. Its "Rule of 40" score, which adds its revenue growth to its
adjusted operating margin, was 158%: growth of 96% plus a margin of 62%.

It also raised its forecasts for the year: adjusted operating income of $5,245 million to $5,249 million, and
adjusted free cash flow of $4.8 billion to $5.0 billion.

### What its chief executive said

In his letter to shareholders, Karp called the quarter's results "extraordinary". He wrote that demand for its
software "has been absolutely ravenous and remains far from sated in the United States", and that sales that once
took "months and years" "can now take days and weeks". He repeated Palantir's aim "to take the entire market,
the whole thing". On the results call, he said: "we really cannot satiate demand."

These are Palantir's views of its own business, not ours.

### How the shares reacted

Palantir closed at $178.34 on 2 November, already down 3.1% that day before the results came out. On 3 November it
opened at $168.18 and was as low as $165.25, down 7.3%, before closing at $175.40, down 1.6%. The Nasdaq Composite
rose 0.8% and the S&P 500 0.4% the same day.

Coverage of the fall pointed to the contract figures. Gotrade reported that the Motley Fool tied the drop to
"slowing contract growth, with total contract value up 6% and remaining performance obligations unchanged at
$4.9 billion".

On 4 November the shares fell again, to $171.70. That leaves them 17.9% below their record close of $209.05, set
on 9 October 2026, and down 3.4% this year.

### How Palantir shares usually react to results

We measured all 24 quarterly results Palantir released from November 2020, after it listed in September 2020, to
August 2026, comparing the close before each release with the close after it.

- The middle move, up or down, was **12.4%**.
- Every one of the 24 moved the shares by 5% or more, and 16 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **21.3%** in May 2022.
- The largest rise was **30.8%** in February 2024.
- The last results, in August 2026, sent the shares up **29.5%**.
- The shares rose after 13 of the 24 and fell after 11.

So this was the smallest results-day move in Palantir's history as a listed company.

### How far Palantir has fallen before

Palantir's shares have fallen 20% or more five times since they listed. These are measured from a record close to
the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 2020 | December 2020 | −22.5% | January 2021 |
| January 2021 | December 2022 | −84.6% | October 2024 |
| December 2024 | January 2025 | −21.1% | January 2025 |
| February 2025 | April 2025 | −40.6% | May 2025 |
| November 2025 | June 2026 | −48.2% | October 2026 |

All five got back to their record. The longest took more than three and a half years from the record. That is a
record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### Legal matters

In 2022, shareholders filed a lawsuit alleging that Palantir had made false and misleading statements about its
business and prospects. A court dismissed it in April 2025, and the shareholders appealed; the appeals court heard
arguments on 16 March 2026.

As of 30 September 2026, Palantir said it was not aware of any pending legal matters expected to have a material
adverse impact on its accounts.

### What comes next

- **Contracts.** Whether the value of new contracts picks up again after this quarter's 6%, and whether
  contracts grow longer again, as Palantir says it expects.
- **The forecast.** Whether fourth-quarter revenue lands in the $2,649 million to $2,653 million Palantir expects.
- **US commercial.** Palantir expects more than $3,559 million of US commercial revenue in 2026, growth of at
  least 143%.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Palantir's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours. The
share-price history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information
