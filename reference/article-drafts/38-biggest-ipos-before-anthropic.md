## Body

### The short version

Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November 2026, after the largest initial public offering on record. Its
shares were sold at $83.00, opened at $111.50, closed the first day at $97.17 and the second at $88.91.

We looked at what happened to every US IPO since 2008 that ranks among the 25 largest ever, using Renaissance
Capital's table. There are 13 before Anthropic. In short:

- **All 13 later closed below their first-day close.** Eight of them did it the very next trading day. Anthropic, the
  fourteenth, did it on its second day too.
- **12 of the 13 closed below the price the banks sold them at**, all within a year of listing. Medline is the only
  one that has not.
- **Day one did not decide the ending.** Five are above their first-day close today, Visa by 2,745% and Facebook by
  1,367%. Eight are below it, Rivian by 89.7%.

These are 13 companies, each listed in its own market. The pattern is a record of what happened, not a forecast for
Anthropic.

### How we chose the 13

The list is every US IPO priced from 2008 onwards in Renaissance Capital's table of the 25 largest US IPOs, by the
amount raised. We did not add or drop any. Anthropic tops the table at $81.3 billion, ahead of SpaceX's $75.0 billion
in June.

Three prices matter on the first day:

- **The IPO price** is what the company and its banks charged the investors they allocated shares to, the night
  before trading began.
- **The first trade** is the opening price on the first day, set by everyone else.
- **The first-day close** is the last price that day. Every comparison below starts from it.

Alibaba, Arm and SK hynix sold American depositary shares, so their prices are per depositary share. Visa's are shown
as they were in 2008, before its four-for-one share split in 2015.

### Visa: listed into a crisis

Visa priced its IPO at $44 on 18 March 2008, two days after JPMorgan Chase announced it would buy Bear Stearns, with the
Federal Reserve agreeing to fund up to $30 billion of Bear Stearns' less liquid assets. The first day closed at $56.50.
The shares first closed below that on 6 October 2008, at $55.38, and closed at $42.42 on 20 January 2009, their
lowest close ever and 24.9% below the first-day close. Today they are 2,745% above it.

### Facebook: the debut that broke

Facebook's first day went wrong at the opening. The SEC later found that "a design limitation in NASDAQ's system to
match IPO buy and sell orders caused disruptions to the Facebook IPO", and Nasdaq paid a $10 million penalty, "the
largest ever against an exchange". It had also set aside up to $62 million to compensate trading firms for the
failures, under a plan the SEC approved.

The shares opened at $42.05, closed the first day at $38.23 and the second at $34.03, below the $38 IPO price. On
16 August 2012, the 91st day, 271,123,815 shares held by early investors became free to sell under Facebook's
prospectus; that day the shares closed at $19.87. On 4 September they closed at $17.73, their lowest, 53.6% below the
first-day close. The first close back above $38 came on 2 August 2013, at $38.05, more than 14 months after the
listing. Today the shares are 1,367% above their first-day close.

### Rivian: up 70.8% in four trading days, then down 89.7%

Rivian's first trade was $106.75, 36.9% above its $78 IPO price, and it closed its first day at $100.73. Four trading
days later it closed at $172.01, 70.8% above that. It first closed below its first-day close on 17 December 2021, at
$97.70, and below its IPO price on 18 January 2022. Its lowest close in the first year was $20.60, 79.5% below the
first-day close, and on 20 November 2026 it closed at $10.33, 89.7% below it.

### SpaceX: the one still running

SpaceX's first trade was $150.00, against an IPO price of $135, and it closed its first day at $160.95. Two trading
days later it closed at $201.80, its highest close. From there:

- **22 June:** first close below its first-day close, at $154.60.
- **16 July:** first close below its IPO price, at $131.11.
- **5 August:** its lowest close, $108.27, 32.7% below its first-day close.
- **5 October:** back above its first-day close, at $171.09.
- **20 November:** $127.79, 20.6% below its first-day close.

Its prospectus releases the shares under its 180-day lock-up in stages, the last of them on 8 December 2026. Its
founder's shares are locked for 366 days.

### What came after: regulators and courts

Several of the 13 have a second story behind the share price, involving regulators, courts or a government. These
are the outcomes on the record:

- **Facebook:** beyond Nasdaq's penalty, the shareholders' lawsuit over the IPO was settled for $35 million in cash.
- **Alibaba:** in January 2015 a paper posted on the website of China's State Administration for Industry and
  Commerce referred to a meeting between Alibaba and regulators in July 2014, two months before its listing.
  Shareholders sued. In April 2019 Alibaba agreed to pay $250 million to settle; the settlement "does not constitute
  an admission or finding that the claims asserted had any merit".
- **Uber:** in 2024 a shareholders' lawsuit over the IPO documents was settled for $200 million.
- **Rivian:** in October 2025 Rivian agreed to pay $250 million to settle a lawsuit brought for buyers of its shares
  between 10 November 2021 and 10 March 2022. It "denies the allegations in the suit".
- **Coupang:** a lawsuit over its IPO was dismissed in September 2025.
- **Lineage:** a lawsuit over its IPO documents was pending in federal court in Michigan at the plaintiffs' lawyers'
  last update, in February 2026.
- **General Motors:** the US Treasury agreed to sell 358,546,795 of its own GM shares in the IPO, at $33. When it sold its last
  shares, on 9 December 2013, it said it had recouped $39 billion from its original investment in GM.
- **Cerebras:** it first filed to list in September 2024, while a US national security committee, CFIUS, was
  reviewing an investment by G42, a partner headquartered in the United Arab Emirates. In March 2025, after G42's
  role was cut back, CFIUS let Cerebras withdraw the notice. Cerebras withdrew its filing in October 2025 and
  listed in May 2026.

### Where Anthropic is

Anthropic closed at $88.91 on 20 November, 8.5% below its first-day close and 7.1% above its $83.00 IPO price. The
first of its 23 lock-up release dates is 15 December 2026, a week after SpaceX's last. Every date is listed in
[Anthropic's lock-up: the 23 dates its shareholders can start selling](/articles/anthropic-lock-up-dates).

### What this cannot tell you

Thirteen companies are a small group, and each listed in a different market, from Visa in a financial crisis in 2008
to SK hynix in 2026. Size is the only thing they share. The tables record what happened
to each of them; they say nothing about what Anthropic's shares will do.
