## The answer, shown under the heading

Apple's revenue rose 12% to $114.5 billion in its September quarter, and it said the December quarter would be
"the best quarter for revenue in the history of the company". But it forecast a lower gross margin, blaming higher
memory costs, and its shares fell 1.9% the next day, while the S&P 500 rose 0.4%.

## Body

### The short version

Apple, which makes the iPhone, the Mac and the iPad and sells services such as the App Store and iCloud, reported
results for the three months to 26 September 2026, the last quarter of its financial year, after the US market
closed on 2 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $114,477 million, up 12% from $102,466 million a year earlier, a record for a September quarter.
- **iPhone:** $57.6 billion, up 17%.
- **Profit:** net income of $30,711 million, up 12%.
- **Gross margin:** 47.9%, which Apple said included about 1 percentage point from tariff refunds, down from 50.1%
  in the June quarter.
- **The forecast:** December-quarter revenue up 7% to 10%, with a gross margin of 45% to 46%.
- **The shares:** down 1.9% the next day, to $331.90.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Three months to Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $114,477M | $102,466M | +12% |
| Products | $82,904M | $73,716M | +12% |
| Services | $31,573M | $28,750M | +10% |
| Gross margin | 47.9% | 47.2% | +0.7 points |
| Operating expenses | $19,189M | $15,914M | +21% |
| Net income | $30,711M | $27,466M | +12% |
| Earnings per share | $2.10 | $1.85 | +14% |

Apple said earnings per share included $0.05 from tariff refunds. Spending on research and development rose 32%,
to $11,730 million. For the full financial year, revenue was a
record $478,834 million, up 15%.

### Where the growth came from

| | Three months to Sep 2026 | Change on a year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| iPhone | $57,583M | +17% |
| Mac | $10,792M | +24% |
| iPad | $6,229M | −10% |
| Wearables, Home and Accessories | $8,300M | −8% |
| Services | $31,573M | +10% |

Services revenue was an all-time high, and Parekh said Apple's "installed base of over 2.5 billion active devices"
had reached another record. By region, sales in Greater China rose 21%, to $17,539 million. Finance chief Kevan Parekh said the quarter had
"supply constraints on iPhone, Mac and iPad", and that iPad also faced "a continued difficult compare" with a new
model launched a year earlier.

### The margin

Apple's gross margin is the share of revenue left after the cost of making what it sold. It had risen for three
quarters in a row before this one.

| Three months to | Gross margin |
|---|---|
| September 2025 | 47.2% |
| December 2025 | 48.2% |
| March 2026 | 49.3% |
| June 2026 | 50.1% |
| September 2026 | 47.9% |
| December 2026 (Apple's forecast) | 45% to 46% |

Chief executive John Ternus said "memory pricing right now is unprecedented", and that for the December quarter
Apple expects "even higher memory costs". Parekh said the margin forecast "includes primarily the impact of higher
memory costs as well as some costs related to the ramp of Siri AI". Ternus said Apple had changed the prices of
many of its products, the Mac and iPad in June and the iPhone in September.

### The forecast

| | December 2025 quarter | Apple's forecast for December 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $143,756M | Up 7% to 10% |
| Gross margin | 48.2% | 45% to 46% |
| Operating expenses | $18,379M | $20.4B to $20.8B |

Growth of 7% to 10% on the December 2025 quarter would be revenue of about $153.8 billion to $158.1 billion, more
than any quarter Apple has reported. Parekh said the forecast "reflects our best view of constrained supply", and
that Apple expects services revenue to grow 8% to 9%. He said operating expenses would grow 11% to 13%, "driven by
increased and continued investment in R&D, in particular, AI".

### How the shares reacted

Apple closed at $338.32 on 2 November. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 3 November the
shares were at $331.90, down 1.9%. The same day, the S&P 500 rose 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.8%.

CNBC reported a "disappointing margin forecast" and said iPad sales had missed analysts' estimates.

The next day, 4 November, big technology shares rallied, and Apple rose 5.3% to $349.51, a record close. CNBC and
Gotrade linked that rise to the US midterm election results, not to Apple's results. By then the shares were up
29% since the end of 2025.

### How Apple shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 results releases before this one, from January 2016 to July 2026. Apple reports after the
market closes, so we compared the close on the next day with the close on the day it reported. We left out the
letter Apple released in January 2019 cutting its revenue forecast, because it was not a results release.

- The middle move, up or down, was **3.5%**.
- 13 of the 43 moved by 5% or more, and 1 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **7.4%** in July 2026, after the last results.
- The largest rise was **10.5%** in July 2020.
- The shares rose after 21 of the 43 and fell after 22.

31 of the 43 moved more than this one.

### How far Apple has fallen before

Apple has fallen 40% or more eight times since 1980, the start of our price history. These are measured from a
record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it, counting dividends:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| December 1980 | July 1982 | −69.4% | January 1983 |
| June 1983 | August 1985 | −76.9% | February 1987 |
| October 1987 | October 1990 | −56.5% | February 1991 |
| April 1991 | December 1997 | −81.2% | September 1999 |
| March 2000 | April 2003 | −81.8% | January 2005 |
| January 2006 | July 2006 | −40.8% | November 2006 |
| December 2007 | January 2009 | −60.9% | October 2009 |
| September 2012 | April 2013 | −43.8% | July 2014 |

All eight got back to their record. The longest took more than eight years from the record; measured from the low,
none took more than two years. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether December-quarter revenue grows 7% to 10%, to a record.
- **The margin.** Whether it lands between 45% and 46%, and what Apple says about memory costs after that.
- **Supply.** Whether the constraints on iPhone, Mac and iPad ease.
- **Spending.** Operating expenses of $20.4 billion to $20.8 billion, as Apple spends more on AI.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Apple's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record of what
happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account your
objectives, financial situation or needs.
