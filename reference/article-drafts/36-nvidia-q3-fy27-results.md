## Body

### The short version

Nvidia, which designs the chips and computer systems that most artificial intelligence runs on, reported its results
for the three months to 25 October 2026, the third quarter of its financial year, after the market closed on
17 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $115.8 billion, up 103% from $57.0 billion a year earlier, and $7.8 billion above the middle of
  Nvidia's own forecast.
- **Data centres:** $107.6 billion of it, up 110%.
- **The forecast:** about $130 billion for the quarter to January. That quarter has an extra week, and per week the
  rise is smaller than it looks.
- **The new boss:** the results were Nvidia's first since Jensen Huang, who co-founded it, resigned as chief
  executive on 12 November. Its finance chief, Colette Kress, is running it until a successor is found.
- **The shares:** down 2.9% on 18 November, to $150.96, after opening higher.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $115.8B | $57.0B | +103% |
| Gross margin | 74.8% | 73.4% | +1.4 points |
| Operating income | $77.5B | $36.0B | +115% |
| Net income | $65.3B | $31.9B | +105% |
| Earnings per share | $2.70 | $1.30 | +108% |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $2.68 | $1.27 | +111% |

Adjusted earnings per share is Nvidia's own measure. Since the start of this financial year it no longer leaves out
the cost of the shares it pays its staff in, so it sits close to the reported figure. This quarter it was slightly
lower, because it leaves out a $1.8 billion net gain on the companies Nvidia has invested in.

### Where the revenue came from

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data centres | $107.6B | $51.2B | +110% |
| of which the largest cloud companies | $60.1B | $35.1B | +71% |
| of which other AI clouds, industry and business | $47.5B | $16.1B | +195% |
| Edge computing (PCs, robots, cars) | $8.2B | $5.8B | +42% |

Nvidia said the growth came from its new Rubin chips, "our fastest architecture ramp ever", its Vera processors, and
Blackwell Ultra. It shipped $2.4 billion of its Hopper data-centre chips to China in the quarter.

### How far it beat its own forecast

In August Nvidia forecast revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, for this quarter. It reported $115.8 billion,
$7.8 billion or 7.3% above the middle, and $5.7 billion above the top of the range.

Analysts had expected adjusted earnings per share of $2.48 and revenue of $109.10 billion, according to LSEG data
reported by CNBC.

### The forecast, and the extra week

Nvidia expects revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, for the quarter to January, against $125.1 billion that
analysts had expected, CNBC reported. That is 12.2% more than this quarter.

But Nvidia says the quarter "includes an additional week": 14 weeks, against 13 in the quarter just reported. Per
week, $130.0 billion is about $9.29 billion, against about $8.91 billion this quarter, a rise of 4.2%.

The forecast assumes no data-centre chip revenue from China. Nvidia expects its gross margin, the share of revenue
left after the direct cost of what it sells, to fall to between 71.0% and 72.0%, from 74.8%.

Looking further ahead, Kress said on the call that Nvidia expects revenue to grow 75% in the year to January 2028,
up from the 70% it had forecast before, CNBC reported.

### A new chief executive, and an investigation

On 8 November Nvidia's board said it was "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that
founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort". It added that it had "no reason
to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised". A committee of independent directors
is overseeing it, with outside lawyers.

Nvidia's filing says the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, chief executive and director on
11 November, effective immediately, and announced it the next day. The board named Kress interim chief executive,
alongside her role as finance chief, which she has held since 2013, and appointed Harvey C. Jones, a director since
1993, as its first chair. Nvidia's results release says the investigation is still going on.

In a statement reported by CNBC on 17 November, Huang said: "I have made serious mistakes in my personal life." He
said he would be "honored" to return if the board decided that was in the company's interest. On the results call,
Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate him, CNBC and NBC News reported.

### Cash, stock and commitments

Nvidia generated $48.3 billion of cash from operations in the quarter, less than its $65.3 billion of net income. The
biggest part of the gap is money it is still owed: what customers owe it rose $18.1 billion in the quarter, to $81.0 billion.
Nvidia said this reflected the timing of Rubin shipments and "extended payment terms on large, multi-quarter
agreements with certain investment-grade customers". Its stock of unsold chips and parts rose to $39.7 billion, from
$31.6 billion three months earlier, for the Rubin ramp.

It has also promised a great deal ahead:

- **Supplies:** $390 billion of commitments to buy parts and capacity, up from $279 billion three months earlier,
  mainly memory for several years ahead.
- **Guarantees:** up to $118.0 billion that Nvidia may have to pay if certain partners fail to meet their own
  obligations. Most of it, up to $105.0 billion, backs the building of data centres in Ohio that will be leased to
  OpenAI. Nvidia says it
  has made no payments under its guarantees so far.

It returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter: $22.1 billion buying back its own shares and $6.0 billion in
dividends, against $243 million in dividends a year earlier. It pays $0.25 a share each quarter.

### How the shares reacted

Nvidia closed at $155.46 on 17 November. On 18 November it opened at $159.19, up 2.4%, then fell as low as $150.40
and closed at $150.96, down 2.9%. The S&P 500 fell 0.2% and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.4% the same day.

The Motley Fool reported that investors "are now watching succession uncertainty and China-related regulatory
scrutiny".

The fall started before the results. Nvidia closed at $186.64 on 6 November, the last trading day before the board's
statement, and has fallen 19.1% since. It is 36.9% below its record close of $239.24, set on 6 October 2026, and
down 19.1% since the end of 2025.

### How Nvidia shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of Nvidia's results releases before this one, from February 2016 to August 2026. Nvidia reports
after the market closes, so we compared the next day's close with the close on the day it reported.

- The middle move, up or down, was **5.3%**.
- 23 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and 7 by 10% or more.
- The shares rose after 25 and fell after 18.
- The largest fall was **18.8%** in November 2018.
- The largest rise was **29.8%** in November 2016.

So a 2.9% fall is a small move for Nvidia on a results day.

### How far Nvidia has fallen before

Counting dividends and allowing for share splits, Nvidia's shares have fallen 50% or more seven times since 1999,
the start of our price history. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got
back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 2000 | April 2000 | −53.9% | June 2000 |
| June 2000 | December 2000 | −67.8% | April 2001 |
| June 2001 | October 2001 | −51.9% | November 2001 |
| January 2002 | October 2002 | −89.7% | November 2006 |
| October 2007 | November 2008 | −85.1% | April 2016 |
| October 2018 | December 2018 | −56.0% | February 2020 |
| November 2021 | October 2022 | −66.3% | May 2023 |

All seven got back to their record. The slowest, the fall that began in 2007, took eight and a half years. The
current fall, 36.9% from the October record, is smaller than any of them so far. That is a record of what happened,
not a guide to what will happen.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether revenue for the quarter to January lands between $126.1 billion and $133.9 billion.
- **The margin.** Whether the gross margin lands between 71.0% and 72.0%, as Nvidia expects.
- **The chief executive.** Who the board names, and when.
- **The investigation.** What the independent committee finds.
- **Cash.** Whether what customers owe Nvidia keeps growing faster than its revenue.

### What this cannot tell you

history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
