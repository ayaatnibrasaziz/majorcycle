## Body

### The short version

Meta reported its results for the three months to 30 September after the US market closed on
21 October 2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $64.60 billion, up 26% on a year earlier, and above the $61–64 billion Meta had
  forecast in July.
- **Profit:** operating income of $12.99 billion, down 37%, after a $9.89 billion charge for its
  agreement with US state attorneys general over teen mental health. Without that charge, Meta
  says operating income would have been $22.88 billion, up 11%.
- **Earnings per share:** $3.83, or $7.40 without the charge.
- **Spending:** $34.91 billion of capital expenditure in the quarter, mostly servers, data centres
  and network infrastructure, which left free cash flow at **negative $2.12 billion**.
- **What's new:** a non-voting Class C share, to be given to every shareholder, one for each share
  they own, after the new class takes effect on 19 January 2027.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against Meta's own forecast

Since October 2021, Meta has given a revenue range for the next quarter with each set of
results, so the cleanest test of a quarter is against that range.

| | Meta's forecast (July) | Reported | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $61–64B | $64.60B | $51.24B |
| Revenue growth | 19–25% (implied by the range) | 26% | 26% |
| Operating income | — | $12.99B | $20.54B |
| Operating margin | — | 20% | 40% |
| Net income | — | $9.86B | $2.71B |
| Diluted earnings per share | — | $3.83 | $1.05 |

Revenue came in 0.9% above the top of the range. Meta's quarterly forecast covers revenue, not
profit, so the profit lines have no forecast to test against.

The year-earlier profit figures need a warning. Both quarters carry a large one-off cost, on
different lines:

- **This year,** the $9.89 billion legal charge sits in expenses. Without it, earnings per share
  would have been $7.40.
- **A year ago,** a one-time, non-cash tax charge of $15.93 billion, from changes to US tax law,
  cut net income to $2.71 billion. Without it, earnings per share would have been $7.25.

So the reported 265% rise in earnings per share is mostly those two charges. On Meta's own figures
without them, earnings per share grew by about 2%, from $7.25 to $7.40.

Revenue growth has held up. It was 24% in the fourth quarter of 2025, 33% in the first quarter of
2026, 28% in the second, and 26% now. Advertising, almost all of Meta's revenue, grew 26%: Meta
showed 10% more ads and charged 14% more for each one on average. On average, 3.64 billion people
used at least one of its apps each day in September, 3% more than a year earlier.

### The legal charge

In August, Meta reached an agreement with 52 attorneys general across US states, territories and
the District of Columbia to settle their lawsuits over teen mental health and social media. It
provides for payments of up to about $18.59 billion over ten years, part of which depends on
other companies, such as YouTube and TikTok, agreeing to similar terms, and changes to how Facebook and Instagram work for
US users under 18.

Meta booked a $9.89 billion charge for the agreement in the third quarter. It also warned that it has "a number of
youth-related trials scheduled in the coming months" in the US.

### Where the money is going

Meta's spending on computing power keeps rising. In the third quarter, capital expenditure was
$34.91 billion, against $31.08 billion in the second quarter and $19.37 billion a year earlier. Cash from
operations was $32.79 billion, so spending ran ahead of it and free cash flow was negative.

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $32.79B | $30.00B |
| Capital expenditure | $34.91B | $19.37B |
| Free cash flow | −$2.12B | $10.62B |

To pay for it, Meta borrowed $25.00 billion in May, and it did not buy back any of its own
shares in the first nine months of the year: $25.03 billion of its buyback authorisation is still unused. It still pays a
quarterly dividend of $0.525 a share. It ended September with $82.45 billion of cash and
marketable securities and $84.00 billion of bonds outstanding.

Its full-year capital expenditure forecast is now $135–145 billion, narrowed from $130–145
billion. Its 2026 expense forecast rose to $175–179 billion, from $165–169 billion, an increase
of $10 billion that Meta says takes in the legal charge. Meta usually gives the next year's forecasts with its
fourth-quarter results, and says it expects "to continue investing aggressively".

### Muse, and why Australia does not have it yet

Most of the call was about Muse, a personal AI agent that can shop, book and manage tasks for its
user. CEO Mark Zuckerberg said that in a month and a half, "more than 10 million people in the US
and Canada have set up Muse personal agents", and that about a quarter of them use it on any
given day.

It is not available in Australia. CFO Susan Li: "today Muse is only available in the U.S. and
Canada". Muse takes a lot of computing power to run, which Zuckerberg named as one reason it has
not launched more widely, and Meta expects to make it more than ten times more cost-efficient
over the next year. For now it makes money only from subscriptions.

### Is Meta splitting its stock?

In effect, yes. Meta is creating a new class of shares, Class C, which carry no votes. Every
shareholder will receive one Class C share for each Class A or Class B share they hold. Li called
it "effectively resulting in a two-for-one stock split".

- **When:** the new share class takes effect on 19 January 2027, and Meta expects Class C trading to
  "begin in late January 2027". The exact record and payment dates are still to be set.
- **What happens to the price:** after the payment date, Meta expects the Class A share price to
  reflect a two-for-one split. In principle a shareholder's holding is worth the same, spread over
  twice as many shares.
- **Votes:** no shareholder's voting power changes. Class C shares have none.
- **Why:** Meta says it wants a share it can use to raise money, pay employees and make
  acquisitions without changing who controls the votes. Li: "We don't have any specific financing
  plans to announce at this time".

How much money might be raised is the open question. The information statement Meta filed with
the results says forecasts management showed the board in May pointed to "the Company's need to
raise hundreds of billions of dollars over a period of several years beginning as early as fall
2026". Asked about it on the follow-up call, Li said the reference was "a theoretical maximum"
and "not actually a description of our present equity financing needs or plans".

Zuckerberg holds about 56.1% of the votes, through his Class B shares. Under a separate
agreement, he can sell 20% of the Class C shares he receives freely, but for the other 80% each
sale must be matched by selling or converting one of his voting shares.

### Two things for Australian readers

Beyond Muse, Meta's quarterly report names Australia twice as a risk:

- **The under-16 social media ban.** It lists "recent legislation in Australia" that "imposes a
  social media ban for users under 16 years old" among the youth rules it must comply with.
- **Paying for news.** It notes that "Australia has enacted a tax on revenue to incentivize
  payments to news publishers".

### How the shares reacted

On 22 October, the first full day of trading after the results, Meta closed at $648.85, down 7.0%
from $697.52 the day before. The shares had risen in after-hours trading on 21 October, by about
3.8% according to Investing.com, before the full session's fall. The wider market fell too: the
S&P 500 lost 1.2% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.6% that day.

It was the third results day in a row on which Meta's shares fell. After the fall, Meta was:

- **17.9% below its record close** of $790.00 on 12 August 2025,
- **16.8% below its highest price of the past year**, $779.82 on 24 September 2026,
- **1.7% lower than at the start of 2026.**

The current dip has already been deeper. From that August record, Meta fell as far as $525.72
on 27 March 2026, 33.5% below.

The US market is still near its high: on 22 October the S&P 500 was 2.0% below its record close,
set on 6 October.

### How Meta shares usually react to results

Results days have become bigger events for Meta. We measured all 44 regular results releases from
January 2016 to October 2026, comparing the close on the day after with the close on the day Meta
reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **6.0%**. Since 2022 it was **9.5%**.
- 13 of the 44 moved by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **26.4%** in February 2022, when Meta forecast revenue growth of just 3–11%
  for the next quarter and pointed to competition for people's time, the shift to Reels, which
  earn less, and Apple's iOS changes. It fell **24.6%** again in October 2022.
- The largest rise was **23.3%** in February 2023, when Meta added $40 billion to its buyback and
  cut its expense forecast.
- The shares fell after 21 of the 44. The last four: January 2026 (+10.4%), April 2026 (−8.6%),
  July 2026 (−8.0%) and October 2026 (−7.0%).

### How far Meta has fallen before

Meta has fallen 30% or more six times since it listed in May 2012, counting the current fall.
These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| May 2012 | September 2012 | −53.6% | August 2013 |
| July 2018 | December 2018 | −43.0% | January 2020 |
| January 2020 | March 2020 | −34.6% | May 2020 |
| September 2021 | November 2022 | −76.7% | January 2024 |
| February 2025 | April 2025 | −34.2% | June 2025 |
| August 2025 | March 2026 (deepest so far) | −33.5% | Not yet |

The first was the slide after its listing; the 2018 fall began with the results day of July 2018
(−19.0%); the 2020 fall came in the market crash of March 2020; and the 2021–22 fall took in both
of the 2022 results-day falls above.

The first five all got back to their record, and how long it took followed how far they fell.
The two falls of about a third, in 2020 and 2025, were back about two months after the low. The
three deeper ones, of 43% to 77%, took between 11 and 15 months. The current fall is, so far,
about the size of the two quick ones. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

Measured on our stock page's setting, Meta normally falls about 21.7% below its one-year high
before turning. On 22 October it was 16.8% below, inside its normal range.

### What comes next

- **The fourth quarter.** Meta forecasts revenue of $73–76 billion, 22–27% more than a year
  earlier, and a tax rate of 15–17%.
- **2027.** No revenue forecast yet. Meta says it expects to "sustain strong overall revenue
  growth throughout 2027", and that Reality Labs, its glasses and headsets unit, will lose about
  $15 billion, down from $18–19 billion in 2026.
- **The share split.** The Class C shares take effect on 19 January 2027.
- **The next results.** Meta has not set a date. Its fourth-quarter results came out on
  1 February 2024, 29 January 2025 and 28 January 2026.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter and Meta's own forecasts for the next. A forecast is a
company's estimate and can be wrong. The share-price history is a record of what happened
before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account
your objectives, financial situation or needs.
