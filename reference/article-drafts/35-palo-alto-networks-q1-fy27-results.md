## Body

### The short version

Palo Alto Networks, which sells cybersecurity software and firewalls to companies and governments, reported its
results for the three months to 31 October 2026, the first quarter of its financial year, after the market closed on
18 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $3,445 million, up 39% from $2,474 million a year earlier, and above analysts' forecasts.
- **What it bought:** a year earlier Palo Alto did not yet own CyberArk or Chronosphere, two companies it bought in
  early 2026 for $24.1 billion between them. Much of the growth comes from them.
- **Profit:** operating income fell to $96 million from $309 million, as costs from those deals rose. Net income
  rose to $482 million, helped by a $457 million tax benefit.
- **The forecast:** revenue for the year to July 2027 of $14.52 billion to $14.57 billion, up from $14.10 billion to
  $14.20 billion. The increase is bigger than the amount by which this quarter beat Palo Alto's own forecast.
- **A new deal:** Palo Alto agreed to buy Applied Compute, an AI start-up, for $4.5 billion in shares.
- **The shares:** up 6.4% on 19 November, to $361.73, while the S&P 500 fell 2.0%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Aug–Oct 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $3,445M | $2,474M | +39% |
| Gross margin | 68.0% | 74.2% | −6.2 points |
| Operating income | $96M | $309M | −69% |
| Net income | $482M | $334M | +44% |
| Earnings per share | $0.57 | $0.47 | +21% |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $1.05 | $0.93 | +13% |

Adjusted earnings per share is Palo Alto's own measure. It leaves out the cost of the shares it pays its staff in,
$0.74 a share this quarter, and an accounting charge for the companies it has bought, $0.34 a share, among other
items. It also replaces the actual tax charge with a long-term rate, which this quarter took out $0.84 a share.

### How much of the growth was bought

Palo Alto completed two large purchases early in 2026. It bought Chronosphere, which helps companies monitor their
software and cloud systems, on 29 January for $3.0 billion. It bought CyberArk, which secures the identities people and software use to log in, on
11 February for $21.1 billion, paying CyberArk's shareholders $45.00 in cash and 2.2005 Palo Alto shares for each of
theirs. Neither was part of Palo Alto a year earlier, so the 39% compares a larger company with a smaller one.

Palo Alto does not publish a combined figure for the quarter, but its annual report does for the year to July 2026.
Counting the two companies as if Palo Alto had always owned them, revenue rose 17% that year, against 25% as
reported.

The results call gave the same split by business:

| Business | Growth as reported | As if the deals had always been in place |
|---|---|---|
| Network and AI security | +20% | No separate figure given |
| Cortex (security operations, now including Chronosphere) | +53% | +40% |
| Identity security (CyberArk) | Not given | +23% |

The deal also enlarged the share count. Palo Alto's diluted shares rose to 838 million from 709 million, up 18%, so
each share has a smaller slice of the profit. That is one reason adjusted earnings per share rose 13% while revenue
rose 39%.

### Why profit fell while revenue rose

The cost of the deals shows up in the accounts. The charge for the value of what it bought, spread over time, rose to
$282 million from $39 million. The cost of the shares it pays its staff in rose to $622 million from $387 million.
Operating income fell 69%.

Net income still rose 44%, because Palo Alto recorded a tax benefit of $457 million against a $78 million tax charge
a year earlier. Its release says the quarter included "a one-time tax adjustment relating to a change in our tax
position on a historical transaction". Before tax, it made $25 million, against $412 million a year earlier.

The gross margin, the share of revenue left after the direct cost of what it sells, fell from 74.2% to 68.0%. On
Palo Alto's adjusted measure it was 75.2%, down 1.7 points. Its finance chief, Dipak Golechha, said on the call that
component costs for its hardware were elevated across the industry and would weigh on the margin all year, and that
newer software services, earlier in their life, cost more to run. Hardware is about 10% of Palo Alto's revenue, he
said.

### Recurring revenue and contracts

Palo Alto's next-generation security annual recurring revenue, the yearly value of its contracts for its newer
products and subscriptions, rose 66% to $9.73 billion. Because it counts every such contract, it also includes
CyberArk and Chronosphere, which a year earlier it did not.

Its remaining performance obligations, the revenue it is owed under signed contracts, rose 35% to $21.0 billion.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts had expected adjusted earnings per share of $0.97 and revenue of $3.31 billion, according to LSEG data
reported by CNBC. Palo Alto reported $1.05 and $3,445 million. For the full year, analysts had expected revenue of
$14.16 billion and adjusted earnings per share of $4.18, below Palo Alto's new ranges.

### What Palo Alto now expects

In September Palo Alto forecast revenue of $3.300 billion to $3.310 billion for this quarter. It reported $3,445
million, about $140 million above the middle of that range. It then raised its forecast for the year by about $395
million at the middle:

| Year to July 2027 | September forecast | November forecast |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $14.10B to $14.20B | $14.52B to $14.57B |
| Adjusted earnings per share | $4.16 to $4.19 | $4.26 to $4.30 |

So Palo Alto now expects about $11.1 billion of revenue from the three quarters still to come, up from about $10.8
billion. For the quarter to January it expects revenue of $3.530 billion to $3.550 billion and adjusted earnings per
share of $1.01 to $1.03. It also raised its forecast for next-generation security recurring revenue, to $11.475
billion to $11.525 billion from $11.075 billion to $11.175 billion.

"Given the current threat environment and demand we see ahead of us, we are materially raising our full year
guidance," Golechha said in the release. None of these forecasts include Applied Compute.

### The Applied Compute deal

Applied Compute helps companies train and run open-weight AI models, ones whose trained settings are publicly
released, on their own data. Palo Alto is paying $4.5 billion in its own shares and expects the deal to close in the
quarter to April 2027, subject to regulatory clearance. Applied Compute's chief executive, Yash Patil, will become
Palo Alto's co-chief AI officer.

On the call, Palo Alto said Applied Compute had passed $100 million of annualised revenue within 18 months of being
founded, and that it aimed for the business to add $1 billion of recurring revenue by its 2029 financial year. It also
said:

- the deal would lower its operating margin by up to 1 percentage point in the year to July 2027 and up to 2 points
  the year after, before returning to the current 29% to 30%;
- its capital spending for the business would total less than $3 billion through its 2030 financial year;
- the price includes about $500 million of cash and prepaid computing that Applied Compute holds.

### Cash

Palo Alto generated $1,929 million of cash from operations in the quarter, up from $1,771 million. Its adjusted free
cash flow, which takes out spending on equipment and adds back payments linked to its acquisitions, was $1,937
million, 56.2% of revenue against 69.2% a year earlier. It bought back no shares in the quarter.

### How the shares reacted

Palo Alto closed at $339.92 on 18 November, before its results. On 19 November it opened at $355.81, rose as high as
$370.00, and closed at $361.73, up 6.4%. The S&P 500 fell 2.0% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.3% the same day. In
after-hours trading on 18 November the shares had been up about 9%, at $368.70, and rival cybersecurity companies
rose with them, CrowdStrike by 5%, Zscaler by 3% and Fortinet by 2%, Reuters reported.

The shares had already risen 9.9% on 18 November, before the results, a day the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.4%. Over
the two days they rose 17.0%, from $309.29 to $361.73.

That leaves them 13.9% below their record close of $419.91 on 6 October 2026, and up 96.4% since the end of 2025.

### How Palo Alto shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of Palo Alto's results releases before this one, from February 2016 to September 2026. Palo Alto
usually reports after the market closes, so we compared the close on the next day with the close on the day it
reported. Two were released before the market opened, in June 2018 and November 2020; for those we compared that
day's close with the day before.

- The middle move, up or down, was **6.8%**.
- 29 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and 12 by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **28.4%** in February 2024.
- The largest rise was **18.6%** in August 2021.
- The shares rose after 23 of the 43 and fell after 20.
- The four results before this one all moved the shares down, most recently by **6.8%** in February, **5.6%** in
  June and **9.3%** in September 2026. The last rise was **3.1%** in August 2025.

So a 6.4% rise is close to Palo Alto's usual move, a little below the middle one: 23 of the 43 earlier results moved
the shares more.

### How far Palo Alto has fallen before

Palo Alto's shares have fallen 30% or more seven times since July 2012, when they began trading and our price
history starts. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 2012 | June 2013 | −44.9% | February 2014 |
| July 2015 | April 2017 | −45.2% | May 2018 |
| September 2018 | November 2018 | −30.8% | February 2019 |
| February 2019 | March 2020 | −48.0% | July 2020 |
| April 2022 | January 2023 | −36.0% | May 2023 |
| February 2024 | February 2024 | −30.5% | October 2024 |
| October 2025 | February 2026 | −36.0% | May 2026 |

All seven got back to their record. The longest took almost three years, from July 2015 to May 2018. The fall that
began in February 2024 came mostly from one day: the 28.4% drop after its results that month.

Since the record close on 6 October 2026 the shares have fallen as low as $309.29, on 17 November, 26.3% below it,
not far enough to count as one of these falls. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.
