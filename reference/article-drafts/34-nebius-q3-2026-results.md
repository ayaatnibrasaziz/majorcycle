## Body

### The short version

Nebius, which rents out computing power for artificial intelligence from its own data centres and those of
partners, reported its results for the three months to 30 September 2026 before the market opened on 12 November.
In short:

- **Revenue:** $980.8 million, up 571% from $146.1 million a year earlier, and up 68% on the quarter before.
- **One-off revenue:** about $325 million of it came from a partnership that Nebius says will not recur.
- **Profit:** a net loss of $37.5 million, smaller than the $119.6 million lost a year earlier.
- **The forecast:** revenue for 2026 at the low end of its $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion range, because some capacity
  run by other companies is late.
- **Spending:** about $27 billion this year, more than the $20 billion to $25 billion it had planned, and $40 billion
  to $45 billion in 2027.
- **The shares:** down 16.8% on 12 November, to $128.64, and down another 9.9% the next day.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $980.8M | $146.1M | +571% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $465.8M | −$5.2M | |
| Income from operations | $50.4M | −$130.2M | |
| Net loss | −$37.5M | −$119.6M | |
| Loss per share | −$0.13 | −$0.50 | |

Adjusted EBITDA is Nebius's own measure of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, which also
leaves out the cost of the shares it pays its staff in. Depreciation, the cost of its equipment spread over its
life, was $354.7 million, 36% of revenue against 68% a year earlier. Part of that fall is an accounting change:
Nebius now spreads the cost of its servers and network equipment over five years rather than four.

### A third of the revenue will not repeat

Nebius said it recognised "approximately $325 million of non-recurring revenue as part of our asset-light model",
in which partners pay for data centres that run Nebius's technology. Its quarterly review gives the exact figure,
$325.1 million, and says such revenues "are by their nature non-recurring and the timing and amounts of such
revenues are hard to predict."

Leaving that out, revenue was about $655.7 million, against $582.3 million in the quarter to June.

### What Nebius said about the rest of 2026

Nebius kept its revenue range of $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion but now expects the low end. It said "some
third-party capacity we expected to come online this year is now scheduled for the first half of 2027", and that
the contracts behind it remain in place. One delay is in New Jersey, where it is changing the power supply to fuel
cells from Bloom Energy.

It also moved back its target for annualised run-rate revenue, its revenue in one month multiplied by 12. It had
aimed for $7 billion to $9 billion by December; it now expects more than $7 billion in February. At the end of
September the figure was $6.4 billion, up from $3.0 billion at the end of June. Nebius said it no longer sees it
as "the right measure of our business", and from 2027 it will guide on revenue and pre-tax earnings instead.

It now expects to spend about $27 billion in 2026, above its range of $20 billion to $25 billion, as it builds
capacity for 2027.

### The first forecast for 2027

| | 2027 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $12 billion to $13 billion |
| Capital spending | $40 billion to $45 billion |
| Customer prepayments | $17 billion to $20 billion |
| Pre-tax profit | Breakeven in the fourth quarter |

Nebius said the revenue range was "approximately four times our 2026 revenue". It expects prepayments and financing
backed by contracts with investment-grade customers to cover 75% to 85% of its 2027 spending.

### Prepayments and contracts

Nebius said customers had committed more than $12 billion in contract value since the start of the third quarter.
Prepayments on deals signed in the quarter covered more than 100% of the spending on the capacity behind them, up
from 50% to 60% on deals signed in the quarter before.

Its remaining performance obligations, the revenue it is owed under signed contracts, were $38.7 billion at the end
of September, up 3% from $37.5 billion at the end of June.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts had expected a loss of $0.63 a share and revenue of $907.2 million, Investing.com reported. Nebius
reported a loss of $0.13 and revenue of $980.8 million. For 2027 analysts had expected revenue of $11.98 billion,
below the middle of Nebius's range.

### Cash

Nebius spent $7,300.4 million on equipment and other assets in the quarter. It ended September with $7,425.6
million of cash, against $3,678.1 million at the end of 2025. In October it agreed a $3.5 billion financing
facility with Citi.

### How the shares reacted

Nebius closed at $154.69 on 11 November, up 15.4% that day. On 12 November it opened at $119.94, fell as low as
$118.81, and closed at $128.64, down 16.8%. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.4% the same day. On 13 November it fell
another 9.9%, to $115.88, and on 16 November it closed at $107.13.

InvestorsHub reported that the new guidance "prompted analysts to reduce their share price targets", while
uncertainty about AI infrastructure companies added to the pressure.

That leaves the shares 62.6% below their record close of $286.69, set on 18 June 2026, though still up 28.0% since
the end of 2025.

### How Nebius shares usually react to results

Nebius, formerly Yandex N.V., saw its shares resume trading on Nasdaq on 21 October 2024. We measured all eight of its results releases since
then, from October 2024 to August 2026. Nebius reports before the market opens, so we compared the close on the day
it reported with the close the day before.

- The middle move, up or down, was **5.6%**.
- The shares rose after 6 of the 8 and fell after 2.
- The largest fall was **7.0%** in November 2025.
- The largest rise was **34.1%** in August 2026, after the last results.

So this 16.8% fall was more than twice the size of any earlier results-day fall.

### How far Nebius has fallen before

Nebius's shares have fallen 30% or more four times since trading resumed. These are measured from a record close to
the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| January 2025 | January 2025 | −37.4% | February 2025 |
| February 2025 | April 2025 | −58.3% | June 2025 |
| October 2025 | February 2026 | −45.5% | April 2026 |
| June 2026 | November 2026 | −62.6% | Not yet |

The three earlier falls all got back to their record, the slowest about six months after it was set. That is a record of a
short history, not a guide to what will happen.

### What comes next

- **Capacity.** Whether the delayed capacity, including New Jersey, comes online in the first half of 2027.
- **2026.** Whether revenue lands at the low end of $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion, as Nebius now expects.
- **Spending.** How Nebius funds $40 billion to $45 billion of spending in 2027, and how much prepayments cover.
- **Profit.** Whether it reaches pre-tax breakeven in the fourth quarter of 2027.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Nebius's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours. The share-price
history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
