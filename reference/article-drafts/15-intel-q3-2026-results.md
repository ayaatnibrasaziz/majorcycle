## Body

### The short version

Intel reported its results for the three months to 26 September after the US market closed on 29 October 2026.
In short:

- **Revenue:** $16.5 billion, up 21% from $13.7 billion a year earlier, and $0.2 billion above the middle of
  Intel's own July forecast.
- **Data centre:** $7.3 billion, up 78%. Intel's chief executive called it "the strongest revenue growth for
  the segment in 15 years".
- **PCs:** $8.1 billion, down 5%, which Intel puts down to higher memory prices and limited IT budgets.
- **The forecast:** fourth-quarter revenue of $15.0 billion to $16.0 billion, a non-GAAP gross margin of 38.0%
  and non-GAAP earnings per share of $0.25, all below the third quarter.
- **The shares:** down 28.6% the next day, to $65.76, their worst day since at least 1980, when our price
  history starts.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $16.5B | $13.7B | +21% |
| Gross margin, non-GAAP | 44.1% | 40.0% | +4.1 points |
| Operating income, reported | $2.5B | $0.7B | |
| Net income, reported | $3.3B | $4.1B | −18% |
| Earnings per share, reported | $0.60 | $0.90 | −33% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $0.52 | $0.23 | +126% |

Two sets of earnings point in opposite directions. Reported earnings per share fell a third, because a year
earlier Intel booked a large one-off gain, worth $1.20 a share, from selling 51% of its Altera business. Intel's
non-GAAP figure leaves out such gains and more than doubled.

### Against Intel's own July forecast

In July Intel forecast third-quarter revenue of $15.8 billion to $16.8 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share
of $0.38. Its slides compare the outcome with the middle of that range:

| | Forecast in July | Actual | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $16.3B | $16.5B | +$0.2B |
| Gross margin, non-GAAP | 42.0% | 44.1% | +2.1 points |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $0.38 | $0.52 | +$0.14 |

Intel's finance chief, Dave Zinsner, said on the call: "This marks the eighth consecutive quarter of exceeding
our financial expectations."

### Data centres up, PCs down

| Quarter | Data centre and AI | Change on a year earlier | PCs (CCPG) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third quarter 2025 | $4.1B | −1% | $8.5B |
| Fourth quarter 2025 | $4.7B | +9% | $8.2B |
| First quarter 2026 | $5.1B | +22% | $7.7B |
| Second quarter 2026 | $6.3B | +59% | $8.9B |
| Third quarter 2026 | $7.3B | +78% | $8.1B |

Data-centre growth has sped up in every one of the last five quarters, and its operating margin rose from 23.4%
to 41.5% over the same five quarters. Intel's 10-Q says that in the third quarter "demand for certain data
center products continued to exceed available supply", held back by an industry-wide shortage of the
substrates its server chips are packaged on.

PCs went the other way. The 10-Q says client demand fell "due to industry-wide impacts from higher component
prices, particularly memory, constrained IT budgets and tighter inventory management", and adds: "We expect these
trends to worsen in Q4 2026 and persist in 2027."

### Why the forecast disappointed

| | Third quarter 2026 | Fourth-quarter forecast |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $16.5B | $15.0B to $16.0B |
| Gross margin, non-GAAP | 44.1% | 38.0% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $0.52 | $0.25 |

At the middle of the range, fourth-quarter revenue would be $1.0 billion lower than the third quarter's, though
still $1.8 billion higher than a year earlier. Intel gave three reasons on its call:

- **The PC market.** Intel now expects PC units to fall about 12% in 2026, with the second half "down more than
  20%", and "roughly 10%" in 2027. It expects PC revenue to fall more than 20% from the third quarter as computer
  makers cut their stock of chips.
- **An accident in Israel.** In mid-October, an accident at Intel's factory in Kiryat Gat "resulted in employee
  casualties and injuries and halted manufacturing operations", its 10-Q says. Intel puts the cost at about $300
  million of fourth-quarter revenue and 2 points of gross margin, and expects a similar revenue hit in the first
  quarter of 2027.
- **Factories running below capacity.** Zinsner said the fall in gross margin "primarily reflects lower overall
  revenue against our fixed cost base".

Data centres are still expected to grow. Intel forecasts data-centre revenue up by a mid-teens percentage from the
third quarter.

### Intel Foundry

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.7B | $4.2B |
| Operating loss | −$1.9B | −$2.3B |
| Sales to outside customers | $411M | |

Intel Foundry, which makes chips for Intel and, it hopes, for other companies, still lost $1.9 billion, though
the loss has narrowed in each of the last three quarters. Most of its revenue comes from Intel itself; sales to
outside customers were $411 million. Intel released the 0.9 version of the design kit for its next process,
Intel 14A, in October, and says it will not name foundry customers.

### Where the money is going

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $5.1B | $2.5B |
| Spent on property and equipment | $4.2B | $3.0B |
| Adjusted free cash flow (Intel's measure) | $1.6B | $0.9B |

In August Intel sold about 242 million new shares at $95 each, raising $23.0 billion before costs, and $22.6
billion after. It ended September with $54.0 billion of cash and short-term investments, and $50.1 billion of
debt.

It plans to spend much more. Zinsner said 2026 capital spending is on track to be $20 billion and 2027's will be
"up strongly", and said of 2027: "So I don't think that this will be a year in which we see positive cash flow."

### A profit that moves with Intel's own share price

Intel has an unusual line in its results. Under its agreement with the US government, shares of Intel are held in
escrow and released to the US Department of Commerce as Intel is paid under its Secure Enclave program. Intel
counts those shares as a liability at their market value. So when its own share price rises, its reported profit
falls, and the reverse. The 10-Q shows a $1.0 billion gain in the third quarter, "driven by a decrease in our
stock price", and a $12.6 billion loss for the first nine months, driven by the year's rise. Intel's non-GAAP
figures leave this out.

### How the shares reacted

Intel closed at $92.07 on 29 October. It reported after the market closed, and by the close on 30 October the
shares were at $65.76, down 28.6%. That was Intel's worst day since at least March 1980, the start of our price
history; the previous worst was a 26.1% fall on 2 August 2024. In August, investors paid $95 a share for
$23.0 billion of new Intel stock.

### How Intel shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 regular results releases from January 2016 to July 2026, comparing the close on the day after
Intel reported with the close on the day it reported:

- The middle move, up or down, was **7.4%**.
- 31 of the 43 moved by 5% or more, and nine by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **26.1%** in August 2024.
- The largest rise was **23.6%** in April 2026.
- The shares rose after 16 of the 43 and fell after 27.

### How far Intel has fallen before

Counting dividends, Intel has fallen 40% or more seven times since 1980, the start of our price history,
counting the current fall. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got
back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| November 1980 | January 1982 | −58.1% | April 1983 |
| July 1983 | August 1986 | −63.3% | April 1987 |
| October 1987 | October 1987 | −57.0% | March 1990 |
| July 1990 | October 1990 | −43.2% | March 1991 |
| August 2000 | October 2002 | −82.2% | March 2018 |
| April 2021 | April 2025 | −70.8% | April 2026 |
| June 2026 | October 2026 | −53.3% | Not yet |

The first six all got back to their record. The quickest was back about five months after its low, in 1991; the
slowest, after the 2002 low, took more than 15 years. The current fall began at a record close of $140.94 on
22 June 2026, and Intel is now 53.3% below it. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The PC market.** Whether Intel's forecast of a 10% fall in PC units in 2027 holds, and when memory prices
  ease.
- **Supply.** Whether Intel can make enough server chips; it expects the industry "to remain supply constrained
  throughout 2027 and into 2028".
- **Israel.** How long the Kiryat Gat factory takes to get back to full production, and what it finally costs.
- **Cash.** How much 2027 capital spending rises, with Intel not expecting positive free cash flow that year.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Forecasts are Intel's own, not ours. The share-price history is a record of
what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not take into account
your objectives, financial situation or needs.
