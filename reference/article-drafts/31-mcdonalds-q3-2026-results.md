## Body

### The short version

McDonald's, which owns or franchises more than 45,000 restaurants in more than 100 countries, reported its results
for the three months to 30 September 2026 before the market opened on 4 November. In short:

- **Sales at its restaurants:** systemwide sales, at every McDonald's whether it owns it or a franchisee does, of
  more than $38 billion, up 4%.
- **Comparable sales:** sales at restaurants open at least 13 months, up 1.5% worldwide but down 0.2% in the US,
  where fewer customers came in.
- **Revenue:** $7,157 million, up just 1%, because McDonald's is selling restaurants to franchisees.
- **Profit:** earnings per share of $3.51, up 10%, or $3.57 leaving out restructuring charges.
- **The plan:** a test of new low-priced meal bundles in about 1,900 US restaurants, with a national launch planned
  for early 2027.
- **The dividend:** raised 4% to $1.93 a share each quarter.
- **The shares:** up 6.5% on 4 November, to $280.22, while the S&P 500 rose 1.6%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $7,157M | $7,078M | +1% |
| Operating income | $3,595M | $3,357M | +7% |
| Net income | $2,486M | $2,278M | +9% |
| Earnings per share | $3.51 | $3.18 | +10% |
| Leaving out charges | $3.57 | $3.22 | +11% |

The charges were mainly restructuring costs from an internal reorganisation McDonald's calls Accelerating the
Organization: $56 million before tax this quarter, or $0.06 a share, against $39 million a year earlier.

### Why revenue grew 1% when sales grew 4%

About 95% of McDonald's restaurants are run by franchisees, who pay it rent and royalties. When McDonald's runs a
restaurant itself, it counts everything the restaurant sells as revenue. When it sells that restaurant to a
franchisee, it counts only the rent and royalties. So selling restaurants shrinks revenue even when the restaurants
sell more.

That is what happened this quarter:

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| From franchised restaurants | $4,487M | $4,363M | +3% |
| Sales at its own restaurants | $2,480M | $2,563M | −3% |
| Other revenue | $189M | $151M | +26% |

Chief financial officer Ian Borden said McDonald's had sold about 175 restaurants to franchisees in the US and its
larger international markets so far this year, and that it aims to have 98% of its restaurants franchised by the
end of 2028.

Selling restaurants also brings in gains, which McDonald's counts in operating income. Its gains on selling
restaurants rose from $35 million to $97 million, so $62 million of the $239 million rise in operating income came
from them.

### The US: fewer customers

| Comparable sales | Jul–Sep 2026 | Apr–Jun 2026 | Jul–Sep 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | −0.2% | +0.8% | +2.4% |
| Large international markets | +1.4% | +1.5% | +4.3% |
| Other international markets | +4.2% | +1.9% | +4.7% |
| All restaurants | +1.5% | +1.3% | +3.6% |

In the US, McDonald's said, fewer customers were largely offset by each spending more. It was the first fall in
US comparable sales since early 2025, Restaurant Business reported. It was also not a surprise: at
its Investor Day in September, Borden had said he expected the US to be "slightly negative" for the quarter after a
slower start, Restaurant Business reported.

Chairman and chief executive Chris Kempczinski said US sales fell slightly in July and August and turned positive
in September, after McDonald's brought back some offers in its app and moved its advertising towards its value
meals. Borden gave examples of the app offers: a $2 breakfast sandwich, and free fries with a $1 purchase.
Kempczinski said he was encouraged by "our baseline traffic trends in September and October".

The large international markets, led by Australia and Germany, grew 1.4%, held back by France. Japan led the other
international markets, and China was weaker.

### The value plan

Kempczinski said low entry prices were "the last piece of the value equation for us that we need to figure out".
Late in October McDonald's began an eight-week test of two kinds of bundle, one where customers mix and match and
one with a set combination, in about 1,900 US restaurants. Franchisees running about 4,000 more have started similar
bundles without waiting for the result, so together more than 40% of US restaurants are offering one.

McDonald's will choose between them on what customers prefer, what works in the kitchen and what makes money for
franchisees, and aims to launch one nationally in early 2027.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected earnings per share of $3.38, leaving out charges, and revenue of
$7.29 billion, CNBC reported. McDonald's reported $3.57 and $7,157 million: more profit, less revenue.

On sales, analysts surveyed by StreetAccount had expected US comparable sales to fall 0.5%, CNBC reported, and
worldwide comparable sales to rise 1.3%. Both came in better.

### The plan McDonald's set out in September

At its Investor Day on 23 September, McDonald's set out a strategy it calls NEXT, including remodelled
restaurants, new drinks and chicken, and new targets for 2030. To help franchisees pay for the changes, it said it
would provide about $8.5 billion of support through 2036, about $5 billion of it by 2030, as rent relief and
capital.

Its main 2030 targets:

- an operating margin in the "low-to-mid 50% range", against 47.6% so far in 2026;
- 1.5 percentage points more of the chicken and drinks markets;
- about 250 basis points of gains in the efficiency of its restaurants, which it says is about $100,000 of cash a
  year for the average US restaurant.

The shares fell 4.8% that day.

### The forecast for 2026

McDonald's does not give a forecast for its earnings per share. For 2026 it expects:

- new restaurants to add about 2.5% to systemwide sales growth, leaving out currency movements;
- an operating margin in the "mid-to-high 40% range";
- capital spending of about $3.7 billion;
- about 2,500 new restaurants worldwide, Borden said, a little below its target of 2,600, and about 2,100 more
  restaurants in total once closures are counted.

### The dividend

McDonald's raised its quarterly dividend 4% to $1.93 a share. That is $7.72 a year, 2.8% of the share price on
5 November. It also spent $1,770 million buying back its own shares in the first nine months of 2026.

### How the shares reacted

McDonald's closed at $263.04 on 3 November. On 4 November it was as low as $267.80 and as high as $281.14, and
closed at $280.22, up 6.5%. The S&P 500 rose 1.6% the same day.

CNBC reported the shares rose "as earnings beat" and US restaurants showed improvement. It also reported that when
an analyst asked, Borden "clarified that he did not say that U.S. same-store sales would be better in the fourth
quarter".

On 5 November the S&P 500 fell 4.4% and McDonald's only 0.8%, to $278.04. That leaves the shares 18.5% below their
record close of $341.06, set on 27 February 2026, and down 9.0% this year. From the record to their lowest close
since, $230.88 on 7 October, they fell 32.3%; they have since risen 20.4% from that low.

### How McDonald's shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of McDonald's quarterly results before this one, from January 2016 to August 2026, leaving out
two 2020 sales updates that were not full results. McDonald's usually reports before the market opens, so we
compared the close on the day it reported with the close the day before. It reported after the close once, in
February 2026, and for that one we used the next day.

- The middle move, up or down, was **1.9%**.
- 4 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and none by 10%.
- The largest fall was **5.0%** in October 2019.
- The largest rise was **6.3%** in October 2018.
- The shares rose after 23 of the 43 and fell after 20.

So this 6.5% rise was McDonald's largest results-day move since at least 2016.

### How far McDonald's has fallen before

Counting dividends, McDonald's has fallen 30% or more eight times since 1966, the start of our price history,
including this year. These are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 1966 | October 1966 | −45.7% | January 1967 |
| May 1968 | July 1969 | −77.4% | January 1972 |
| December 1972 | October 1974 | −72.3% | November 1981 |
| August 1987 | October 1987 | −40.2% | May 1989 |
| July 1990 | October 1990 | −33.6% | December 1991 |
| November 1999 | March 2003 | −73.6% | December 2006 |
| September 2019 | March 2020 | −36.9% | September 2020 |
| February 2026 | October 2026 | −31.0% | Not yet |

Counting dividends, this year's fall is 31.0%, smaller than the 32.3% in the share price alone. The seven earlier
falls all got back to their record; the longest took more than eight years. That is a record of what happened, not
a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The US.** Whether US comparable sales stay positive after September, which McDonald's has not promised.
- **Value.** Which bundle wins the test, and how it does when it launches nationally in early 2027.
- **NEXT.** How many franchisees take up the remodels, and what they cost.
- **Selling restaurants.** More gains and lower revenue as McDonald's moves towards 98% franchised.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one quarter. Targets and expectations are McDonald's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours.
The share-price history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information
and does not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
