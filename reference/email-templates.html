MajorCycle — Transactional email templates

Every email MajorCycle sends, rendered exactly as it arrives. Part 1 is the eleven the app sends itself; Part 2 is the thirteen Supabase sends for signing in; Part 3 is the one alert the system sends to us. Twenty-five in all, in one house style.

Source of truth: the code, not this file. These render from web/lib/email/*, web/app/(public)/contact/actions.ts and web/app/api/resend/webhook/route.ts. Every email shares one brand wrapper — renderBrandEmail() in brandEmail.ts (navy→blue gradient header, brand mark, disclaimer footer). The header mark and wordmark below are the real email-icon.png and email-wordmark.png embedded, exactly as production emails render them (the wordmark is an image so GmailThe header logo below is the real email-icon.png embedded, exactly as production emails render it.rsquo;s dark mode cannot darken it, 2026-10-13). Sample names, dates and addresses are illustrative.

How it is set up

Who composes the message

Two senders, and the split is not arbitrary. The app writes anything about the subscription or the account — a trial starting, a card declining, a friend being invited. Supabase writes anything about signing in, because it is the thing that knows a password was reset or an address confirmed.

Who delivers it

Resend, for all twenty-five. The app calls its API directly; Supabase hands its finished messages to the same account over SMTP. So every email leaves from noreply@majorcycle.com — except the contact-form notification, which comes from the monitored support@ inbox and can be replied to.

Where the wording lives

All twenty-five are built by renderBrandEmail() in web/lib/email/brandEmail.ts, so they share one header, one body style and one footer. Part 1 and Part 3 are sent by the app. The thirteen in Part 2 are written in web/lib/email/authTemplates.ts, built into reference/supabase-email-templates/ by pnpm build:auth-emails, and pasted by hand into Supabase under Authentication → Emails → Templates (since 2026-10-13; before that they existed only in the dashboard).

What that means in practice

Part 1 is covered by e2e/email-render.spec.ts, which renders all eleven on every build and checks their links, their figures and this footer. e2e/auth-email-templates.spec.ts checks the Part 2 files against their source. Nothing can check the dashboard itself: after changing a sign-in template, paste the rebuilt file in, or the two will differ.

Every email is written twice

An HTML version and a plain-text one, for readers whose mail client shows no formatting. The previews below are the HTML; each spec table names where the plain-text copy sends people, because the two are written separately and are the easiest thing in this system to let drift apart.

Delivery and authentication

The domain is verified with Resend. Messages are signed DKIM on majorcycle.com and return to send.majorcycle.com; DMARC is set to p=reject with strict alignment, and real mail passes it. security@ receives the authentication reports.

The footer line is identical in all twenty-five: © MajorCycle · Educational information only — not financial advice. It carries no year on purpose — thirteen of the twenty-five are static templates that cannot compute one, and a year would be right in twelve places and frozen in thirteen.

The 4th paragraph says what deleting does to this subscription, from one rule (web/lib/deletionSubscription.ts) that the delete card and /reactivate also read. Unless the last payment failed (then it is cancelled at once, see below), deleting does not cancel the subscription outright: it is set not to renew, and signing back in within the 30 days undoes that. The 3rd paragraph already says to sign back in before the date, so this one never repeats it (2026-10-12). Shown above: monthly, period ends before the deletion date. The others:
· period runs past the deletion date (an annual plan) — “Your subscription won't renew. Your paid period runs past the deletion date, so it ends when the account is deleted, and the unused time isn't refunded. If you come back before then, it carries on as before.”
· trial — “Your free trial stays active until its normal end date, with no charge. If it ends before you come back, you'll return to a free account.”
· payment failed — “Your last payment hasn't gone through, so deleting ends your subscription straight away and we won't try that payment again. If you come back, your account will be on the free plan.”
· payment dispute open — “Your subscription is on hold while a payment dispute is open, and it won't renew.”
· no subscription — the paragraph is left out.
Until 2026-10-03 every paying customer was told their plan “stays valid until the end of the period you’ve already paid for — deleting doesn’t cut it short”, which was untrue for the last three. Since 2026-10-07 deleting with a failed payment cancels that subscription at once, so the unpaid bill is never retried.

Part 2 — the thirteen Supabase sends

Written in web/lib/email/authTemplates.ts with the same wrapper and helpers as Part 1, built into reference/supabase-email-templates/, pasted into the Supabase dashboard, and handed to Resend over SMTP. Each preview is the committed template with Supabase’s own placeholders filled in — {{ .SiteURL }}, {{ .TokenHash }}, {{ .Token }} and {{ .Email }} become a sample link, code and address. All thirteen were pasted and read back identical on 2026-10-13.

The site itself triggers only three of the thirteen (checked against the code, 2026-10-13): Confirm sign up (signing up with email and password), Reset password (“Forgot password”) and Password changed (changing it on /account or after a reset). Nothing on the site offers a passwordless sign-in or a way to change your email address, so Magic link, Change email address and Email address changed fire only if someone calls Supabase directly. MajorCycle has no phone sign-in, no two-factor and no invitation flow, and asks no one to re-confirm a password, so the rest never fire. Sign-in method linked is the one to watch: Supabase links a Google sign-in to an existing account with the same address on its own, and all seven security alerts are switched on in the dashboard (checked 2026-10-13), so a customer who signed up with a password and later uses Google may well receive it. They are kept correct regardless: the template nobody has met is the one most likely to be wrong when someone finally does.
One layout for all twenty-five, since 2026-10-13. Until then these thirteen had drifted from Part 1: slate-grey body text, a fine-print grey too faint to read (2.56:1), a different button, a 480px card for six of them and the title in the body. The owner saw the difference in a real inbox. They now share Part 1’s 600px card, header (the title sits under the wordmark), body text, button, fine print and footer, word for word as before. The security alerts’ pink warning box became a sentence with a bold lead-in, the same as Part 1 (owner’s choice).
