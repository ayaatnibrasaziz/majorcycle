## Body

### The short version

Eli Lilly reported its results for the three months to 30 September before the US market opened on 29
October 2026. In short:

- **Revenue:** $24.4 billion, up 39% from $17.6 billion a year earlier. Volume rose 53% and realized prices
  fell 15%.
- **Mounjaro and Zepbound:** $10.7 billion and $5.4 billion, up 65% and 49%. On their own they brought in
  two dollars of every three Lilly earned.
- **Profit:** net income of $6.5 billion, up 16%. Non-GAAP earnings per share rose only 7%, to $7.48, after
  $4.57 a share of charges for research Lilly bought from other companies.
- **The outlook:** 2026 revenue of $91 billion to $92 billion, up from $85 billion to $87 billion in August and
  $80 billion to $83 billion in February.
- **The shares:** up 6.6% on the day, to a record close of $1,373.70.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $24.4B | $17.6B | +39% |
| Gross margin | 83.9% | 82.9% | +1.0 points |
| Research and development | $4.2B | $3.5B | +23% |
| Marketing, selling and administrative | $3.1B | $2.7B | +14% |
| Acquired research (IPR&D) charges | $4.2B | $0.7B | |
| Operating income | $8.6B | $7.4B | +17% |
| Net income | $6.5B | $5.6B | +16% |
| Earnings per share, reported | $7.22 | $6.21 | +16% |
| Earnings per share, non-GAAP | $7.48 | $7.02 | +7% |

Revenue grew more than twice as fast as profit. The gap is the line Lilly calls acquired in-process research
and development: when it buys a company or a drug that is still in trials, it charges the cost straight to
profit. That charge was $4.2 billion this quarter, mostly for AtaiBeckley and Curevo, against $656 million a
year earlier. It cost $4.57 a share, and Lilly does not add it back in its non-GAAP figures.

Lilly also says the quarter's revenue included about $900 million of benefits, mainly $410 million of milestone payments on Jardiance, a diabetes medicine it sells with Boehringer
Ingelheim, and "favorable adjustments to rebate and discount estimates". It says no further Jardiance
milestones remain.

### Selling more, for less

| | Revenue | Change | Price | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | $15.1B | +34% | −7% | +41% |
| Outside the United States | $9.3B | +47% | −29% | +76% |
| Total | $24.4B | +39% | −15% | +53% |

Lilly sold more of its medicines for less money each. Outside the US, prices fell 29%, mostly because China
added Mounjaro to its national reimbursement drug list, which lowers the price and raises the number of
patients. In the US, prices fell 7%, which Lilly puts mainly down to Zepbound.

This is not new. Lilly's realized prices have been lower than a year earlier in each of the last five quarters:

| Quarter | Revenue growth | Volume | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Third quarter 2025 | +54% | +62% | −10% |
| Fourth quarter 2025 | +43% | +46% | −5% |
| First quarter 2026 | +56% | +65% | −13% |
| Second quarter 2026 | +48% | +60% | −13% |
| Third quarter 2026 | +39% | +53% | −15% |

Growth is still very fast, but it has come down from 56% in the first quarter, and the fall in prices is the
deepest of the five.

### Mounjaro, Zepbound and the rest

| | Third quarter 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mounjaro | $10.7B | $6.5B | +65% |
| Zepbound | $5.4B | $3.6B | +49% |
| Foundayo | $184M | none | new |
| Kisunla | $193M | $70M | +175% |
| Jaypirca | $228M | $143M | +59% |
| Ebglyss | $202M | $127M | +59% |

Mounjaro, for type 2 diabetes, brought in $5.0 billion in the US, up 41%, and $5.7 billion elsewhere, up 93%.
Zepbound, the same medicine, tirzepatide, sold for weight loss, brought in $5.3 billion in the US, up 48%.
Foundayo is Lilly's weight-loss pill, launched in the US in the second quarter; Lilly says it is the only
approved GLP-1 pill "that can be taken any time of day, without food and water restrictions". Its slides say
Foundayo's "share of new patient starts exited Q3 at ~40%".

Its 10-Q puts the concentration plainly: Mounjaro, Zepbound and Foundayo "accounted for 66 percent of our total
revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026".

### Against Novo Nordisk

Lilly's slides, using IQVIA prescription data, put its share of US prescriptions for this class of medicines,
the incretins, at 63.0% in the four weeks to 25 September, against 36.6% for Novo Nordisk, which makes Ozempic and
Wegovy. Outside the US, measured by sales over July and August, Lilly's share was 58.4%. The US market grew 32%
in the quarter and the international market 53%.

In the US, a Medicare program, the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, that opened on 1 July lets Medicare patients buy Lilly's weight-loss medicines at
a discount until the end of 2027. Lilly says more than 500,000 patients are using its medicines through it,
and that 7 in 10 new patients in the program chose Lilly. Monthly prescriptions rose from 222,000 in July to
527,000 in September.

### The outlook, raised again

| Released | 2026 revenue outlook |
|---|---|
| February | $80 to $83 billion |
| April | $82 to $85 billion |
| August | $85 to $87 billion |
| October | $91 to $92 billion |

Lilly has raised its revenue outlook at every results release this year, three times in all. The middle of
the range is now $91.5 billion, $10 billion higher than in February. Its outlook for non-GAAP earnings per share
went the other way, to $35.00 to $36.00, from $35.50 to $36.50: it raised the underlying figure by $4.07 at the
midpoint, then took off the $4.57 of acquired research charges. The outlook leaves out any such charges after
30 September.

### Where the money is going

| First nine months | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations | $27.4B | $13.6B |
| Spent on property and equipment | $8.4B | $5.3B |
| Paid for acquisitions and research deals | $18.2B | |
| Dividends | $4.6B | $4.0B |
| Shares bought back | $4.3B | $2.6B |

Cash from operations doubled. But Lilly spent more than it brought in. Its 10-Q says it paid $18.2 billion for
business development in the first nine months, "primarily driven by the acquisitions of" Centessa, Kelonia,
AtaiBeckley and Orna, among others. On top of that came $8.4 billion on property and equipment, and $9.0 billion
returned to shareholders as dividends and buybacks.

The gap was borrowed. Total debt rose $7.0 billion this year, to $49.5 billion, and cash fell to $4.9 billion.
Lilly started building a $6.5 billion plant in Houston and a $5 billion plant in Richmond, Virginia, this
quarter, two of the ten US sites it has announced since 2020.

### The pipeline

Lilly's next weight-loss medicine, retatrutide, was tested head to head against Ozempic at its highest
approved dose, 2 mg, in adults with type 2 diabetes. After 80 weeks, its slides show weight loss of 20.1 kg and
21.5 kg on the two retatrutide doses against 9.5 kg on Ozempic, and a fall in blood sugar, measured as A1C, of
2.2 points against 1.8. More people stopped taking retatrutide because of side effects: 5% to 8%, against 3%
on Ozempic.

The FDA also approved Mounjaro to reduce cardiovascular risk in adults with type 2 diabetes, and a once-weekly
insulin, Onswik.

### Risks Lilly names

- **Prices.** The 10-Q says US efforts to align drug prices "with international benchmarks" "may result in
  reduced revenue in certain markets". The US government has already picked Jardiance for set prices from 2026,
  and Trulicity and Verzenio from 2028.
- **Concentration.** Two thirds of revenue comes from three medicines built on the same idea.
- **Copies.** Lilly says it continues to see counterfeit and "mass-compounded" versions of its medicines sold.

### How the shares reacted

Lilly closed at $1,288.30 on 28 October. It reported before the market opened the next day, and by the close on
29 October the shares were at $1,373.70, up 6.6%, a record close. Of the 41 earlier results measured below, seven were
followed by a bigger move, in either direction.

### How Lilly shares usually react to results

We measured all 41 regular results releases from July 2016 to August 2026, comparing the close on the day
Lilly reported, before the market opened, with the close the day before:

- The middle move, up or down, was **3.3%**.
- 12 of the 41 moved by 5% or more, and four by 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **14.1%** in August 2025.
- The largest rise was **14.9%** in August 2023.
- The shares rose after 19 of the 41 and fell after 22.

### How far Lilly has fallen before

Counting dividends, Lilly has fallen 35% or more five times since 1972, the start of our price history. These
are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| July 1973 | September 1977 | −60.8% | June 1983 |
| September 1987 | October 1987 | −40.7% | March 1989 |
| January 1992 | August 1993 | −46.4% | March 1995 |
| March 1999 | March 2000 | −43.3% | June 2000 |
| August 2000 | March 2009 | −68.3% | October 2014 |

All five got back to their record. The quickest was back about four months after its low, in 2000; the
slowest, after the 1977 low, took almost six years. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what
will.
