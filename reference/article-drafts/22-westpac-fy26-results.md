## Body

### The short version

Westpac, one of Australia's four big banks, reported its results for the year to 30 September 2026 before the
market opened on 2 November. In short:

- **Profit:** net profit excluding Notable Items, the measure Westpac uses for its own performance, of
  $6,992 million, against $6,972 million a year earlier. That is flat.
- **Statutory profit:** $6,813 million, down 1% from $6,916 million. The gap is $179 million after tax of
  Notable Items, mostly costs from selling its RAMS home-loan book.
- **Bad debts:** an impairment charge of $864 million, up 104% from $424 million.
- **The dividend:** a final dividend of 77 cents, fully franked, the same as the interim, for 154 cents for the
  year, up from 153 cents.
- **The shares:** up 1.0% on the day, to $37.19, while the S&P/ASX 200 fell 0.6%.

All figures are in Australian dollars.

### The year against a year earlier

These figures leave out Notable Items, as Westpac reports them.

| | Year to Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net interest income | $19,687M | $19,473M | +1% |
| Net operating income | $22,708M | $22,464M | +1% |
| Operating expenses | $11,781M | $11,916M | −1% |
| Pre-provision profit | $10,927M | $10,548M | +4% |
| Impairment charges | $864M | $424M | +104% |
| Net profit excluding Notable Items | $6,992M | $6,972M | Flat |
| Statutory net profit | $6,813M | $6,916M | −1% |
| Dividend per share | 154c | 153c | +1% |

Before bad debts and tax, profit rose 4%, because costs fell while income rose. The higher charge for bad debts
took all of that gain away.

### The bad debts

The charge was 10 basis points of Westpac's average gross loans, against 5 a year earlier. Westpac put the increase
down to a "the revised economic outlook, new portfolio overlays and an increase in new IAPs", its
individually assessed provisions against particular customers. It also said it had set aside new overlays "for energy-intensive sectors
and discretionary spending", given economic and geopolitical uncertainty.

The charge was lower in the second half, $421 million, than in the first, $443 million. And Westpac said its
stressed loans fell, from 1.28% of its exposures to 1.17%.

### What analysts had expected

Before the results, analysts' average forecast for profit excluding one-off items was $6.96 billion, according to
ABC News. Westpac reported $6,992 million, slightly above it.

Analysts did not all see it that way. JP Morgan's bank analyst, Andrew Triggs, said the result was "softer than expected", with
a "0.7% miss" on profit excluding Notable Items, "driven by weakness in core NIM", its core net interest
margin. UBS's bank analyst, John Storey, said "weaker revenue
trends" in the second half "were offset by cost discipline and lower than expected credit impairment charges".

### The margin

Westpac's net interest margin, what it earns on its loans less what it pays for its funding, was 1.87% for the
year, down from 1.94%. Analysts had expected 1.89%, according to ABC News. The margin was lower in the second half,
1.86%, than in the first, 1.89%.

### Where its profit came from

Profit before bad debts and tax, by division:

| Division | Year to Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer | $3,616M | $3,437M | +5% |
| Business & Wealth | $3,755M | $3,383M | +11% |
| Institutional | $2,342M | $2,161M | +8% |
| Westpac New Zealand | $1,298M | $1,472M | −12% |

(Westpac calls this pre-provision profit. The New Zealand figure is in Australian dollars.)

Its loans grew 5% over the year, to $901.3 billion, and its deposits 6%, to $766.2 billion. Business lending grew
12% and institutional lending 18%.

### Next year, in Westpac's words

Chief executive Anthony Miller said the economy was "holding up relatively well", but that "inflation remains above
the Reserve Bank's target range and interest rates are likely to remain elevated into 2027".

Westpac's economists expect Australia's economy to grow 1.5% in 2026 and 1.6% in 2027, the unemployment rate to
be 4.7% at the end of both years, and the Reserve Bank's cash rate to be 4.85% at the end of 2026.

### Its capital

Westpac's common equity tier 1 ratio, the main measure of a bank's capital cushion, was 12.46% at 30 September,
against its own target of 11.25%. Westpac says that is $2.9 billion of capital above the target after paying the
final dividend.

### How the shares reacted

Westpac closed at $36.82 on Friday 30 October. It released its results before the market opened on Monday
2 November, and the shares closed that day at $37.19, up 1.0%. The S&P/ASX 200 fell 0.6% the same day.

The other big banks fell. Commonwealth Bank fell 1.0%, ANZ fell 0.6%, and NAB, which reported three days later,
fell 1.4%.

On 5 November, Westpac's shares went ex-dividend: from then, buyers no longer received its 77-cent final
dividend. They closed that day at $36.64, which leaves them 14.7% below their record close, set on 25 February
2026.

### How Westpac shares usually react to results

We measured all 21 half-year and full-year results Westpac released from May 2016 to May 2026, comparing the close
on the day of each release with the close the day before.

- The middle move, up or down, was **2.3%**.
- Only two of the 21 moved by 5% or more.
- The largest fall was **7.4%** in November 2021.
- The largest rise was **5.0%** in May 2021.
- The shares rose on 12 of the 21 days, fell on 8, and closed unchanged on one, in November 2019.

So this was a smaller move than usual: 15 of those 21 results moved the shares further.

### How far Westpac has fallen before

Westpac's shares have fallen 25% or more five times since 1988, the start of our price history. These are measured
from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it, counting dividends:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| February 1990 | October 1992 | −42.8% | December 1993 |
| November 2007 | January 2009 | −49.2% | March 2010 |
| April 2010 | September 2011 | −27.5% | August 2012 |
| March 2015 | September 2015 | −25.0% | March 2017 |
| May 2017 | March 2020 | −51.7% | February 2024 |

All five got back to their record. The longest took almost seven years from the record, and almost four from the
low. Counting dividends, the shares are now 11.1% below their February 2026 record. That is a record of what
happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **Bad debts.** Whether the second half's lower charge continues, as Westpac adds provisions for businesses.
- **The margin.** Whether the fall in its net interest margin, which analysts focused on, continues.
- **Costs.** Whether costs keep falling while Westpac spends more on UNITE, its programme to simplify the bank.
- **The dividend.** The final dividend of 77 cents is paid on 21 December 2026.

### What this cannot tell you

These are results for one year. Forecasts are Westpac's own, or analysts' as reported, not ours. The share-price
history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
