## Body

### The short version

Astera Labs, which makes the chips that move data between the processors, memory and racks inside artificial
intelligence data centres, reported its results for the three months to 30 September 2026 after the market closed
on 3 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $584.9 million, a record, up 154% on a year earlier and up 49% on the quarter before.
- **Its biggest product:** its Scorpio fabric switches became its largest product family.
- **Profit:** net income of $182.9 million, and earnings per share of $0.99. On Astera's own adjusted measure,
  earnings per share were $1.32.
- **The forecast:** revenue of $730 million to $750 million for the three months to December.
- **The shares:** down 4.4% on 4 November, to $223.27, while the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.4%. Then down a further
  19.6% on 5 November, to $179.47.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $584.9M | $230.6M | +154% |
| Operating income | $179.1M | $55.4M | +223% |
| Net income | $182.9M | $91.1M | +101% |
| Earnings per share | $0.99 | $0.50 | +98% |

Against the three months to June, revenue rose 49%, from $392.4 million. Its gross margin was 72.3%, against 76.2% a
year earlier, and its operating margin 30.6%, against 24.0%.

On Astera's own adjusted measure, operating income was $261.6 million, a margin of 44.7%.

Its chief executive, Jitendra Mohan, said growth was "led by Scorpio, which ramped to high-volume production and
became our largest product family by revenue".

### Its products and customers

Astera sells several families of chips, and said on its results call that each one widened its list of customers:

- **Scorpio**, its fabric switches, which connect the processors inside an AI system. Its chief executive said
  "Scorpio represented over $1 billion in annualized revenues". The X-Series shipped to two customers in the
  quarter, and the P-Series to two US cloud companies.
- **Aries**, which keeps signals clean over longer distances, grew on demand for the newest PCIe Gen 6 standard.
- **Taurus**, for faster network cables, is shipping in volume to its lead customer and being tested by several
  more.
- **Leo**, its memory controllers, has passed testing at several customers, including two of the largest cloud
  companies, with volume shipments expected from mid-2027.

Its finance chief, Desmond Lynch, said: "Revenue also diversified across a broader base of customers and platforms
during the quarter."

### Against what analysts expected

Before the results, analysts had expected revenue of $550.8 million and adjusted earnings of $1.19 a share,
according to Investing.com. Astera reported $584.9 million and $1.32.

For the three months to December, Astera forecast revenue of $730 million to $750 million. At the midpoint, that
would be 27% more than in the third quarter. It expects a gross margin of about 72%, and earnings per share of
about $1.13 to $1.17.

### Profit, cash and the forecast

Astera's adjusted gross margin was 72.4%, above the 72% it had forecast. Its adjusted operating costs were
$162 million, up 19% on the quarter before, which it said reflected more spending on research. It ended September
with $1.38 billion in cash and marketable securities, up $125.3 million from June, helped by a record $179.2 million
of cash from its operations.

For the three months to December, besides the revenue range above, it expects an adjusted operating margin of about
45% and adjusted earnings of $1.62 to $1.66 a share.

On the call, Investing.com reported, "Analysts also pressed on whether Scorpio X could plateau before newer
protocols such as UALink take over." Executives said it remains on track, while Astera expands into new platforms
and customers.

### What its chief executive said

Jitendra Mohan called it "an important milestone in our journey to be a critical rack-scale AI infrastructure
solutions provider". He added: "Our design-in activities picked up significantly in Q3 at both new and existing
customers."

These are Astera's views of its own business, not ours.

### How the shares reacted

Astera's shares had already fallen before the results: from $290.43 on 29 October to $233.62 at the close on
3 November, the day it reported.

On 4 November the shares were as low as $208.40, before closing at $223.27, down 4.4%. The Nasdaq Composite rose
1.4% that day, and the S&P 500 1.6%.

On 5 November they fell another 19.6%, to $179.47, on a day the whole market fell: the Nasdaq Composite was down
5.5% and the S&P 500 4.4%. Over the two days, the shares fell 23.2%, almost a quarter of their value.

That leaves the shares 62.8% below their record close of $483.02, set on 30 June 2026, though still up 7.9% this
year.

### How Astera shares usually react to results

We measured all 10 quarterly results Astera released from May 2024, after it listed in March 2024, to August 2026,
comparing the close on the day of each release with the close the next day.

- The middle move, up or down, was **11.4%**.
- Seven of the 10 moved the shares 5% or more, and six 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **21.4%** in February 2026.
- The largest rise was **37.7%** in November 2024.
- The shares rose after 3 of the 10 and fell after 7.

So the 4.4% fall after these results was smaller than usual: seven of the 10 earlier results moved the shares more.

### How far Astera has fallen before

Astera's shares have fallen 20% or more five times since they listed, counting this one. These are measured from a
record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 2024 | April 2024 | −24.1% | April 2024 |
| April 2024 | August 2024 | −57.6% | November 2024 |
| December 2024 | April 2025 | −63.7% | August 2025 |
| September 2025 | March 2026 | −60.2% | May 2026 |
| June 2026 | November 2026 | −62.8% | Not yet |

The four earlier falls all got back to their record, the longest after about eight months. That is a record of
what happened, not a guide to what will.

### Customers and legal matters

Astera relies on a small number of customers. One, which it calls Customer A, accounted for 53% of its revenue in
the quarter, against 23% a year earlier, and its three largest together for 77%. Astera said some of these are
manufacturing partners that buy its chips on behalf of the companies that use them.

It said it is not currently a party to any material legal proceedings.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether revenue for the three months to December lands within the $730 million to $750 million
  Astera expects.
- **Scorpio.** Whether its largest product family keeps growing.

history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
