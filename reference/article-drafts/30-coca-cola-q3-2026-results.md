## Body

### The short version

Coca-Cola, which sells the concentrate for its drinks to bottlers around the world and owns some bottlers itself,
reported its results for the three months to 2 October 2026 before the market opened on 27 October. In short:

- **Revenue:** $13,380 million, up 7% from $12,455 million a year earlier.
- **Volume:** the number of drinks it sold, measured in unit cases, up 5%, the same as the quarter before.
- **Profit:** earnings per share of $0.93, up 8%. On Coca-Cola's own adjusted measure, which it calls comparable
  earnings per share, $0.91, up 11%.
- **North America:** volume flat, which finance chief John Murphy put down to the timing of the Fourth of July and
  a shortage of Topo Chico, the sparkling water Coca-Cola owns, CNBC reported.
- **The forecast:** comparable earnings per share now expected to grow at least 11% in 2026, up from 9% to 10%. It
  started the year at 7% to 8%.
- **The shares:** down 1.6% on 27 October, to $91.18, while the S&P 500 rose 0.6%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $13,380M | $12,455M | +7% |
| Operating income | $4,465M | $3,982M | +12% |
| Net income | $4,009M | $3,696M | +8% |
| Earnings per share | $0.93 | $0.86 | +8% |
| Comparable earnings per share | $0.91 | $0.82 | +11% |

Its operating margin, operating income as a share of revenue, rose from 32.0% to 33.4%. Coca-Cola said currency
movements added 2 points to the growth in earnings per share.

Comparable earnings per share leave out items Coca-Cola treats as one-offs. This quarter's reported figure
included a tax benefit worth $0.04 a share and other costs worth $0.02, so the comparable figure is the lower one.

### Where the growth came from

Coca-Cola reports by region. Organic revenue leaves out currency movements and businesses bought or sold, so it
shows the growth of the business it already had.

| | Revenue | Reported change | Organic change | Volume change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America | $5,386M | +3% | +3% | 0% |
| Europe, Middle East & Africa | $3,104M | +4% | +7% | +5% |
| Latin America | $1,958M | +25% | +16% | +6% |
| Asia Pacific | $1,563M | +4% | +6% | +6% |
| Bottling investments | $1,523M | +13% | +12% | +8% |

Volume growth was led by India, China, Mexico and Brazil, Coca-Cola said. Coca-Cola Zero Sugar grew 16% and Diet Coke
6%. Its sports drinks fell 3%.

Higher prices added 4 points to revenue, Murphy said on the results call, but a less favourable mix, mainly of
countries, the timing of investments and the kinds of drinks sold, took 3 points off. So price and mix together
added just 1%.

### What concentrate sales are, and why they matter

Coca-Cola sells concentrate to bottlers, which turn it into the drinks people buy. Its concentrate sales grew
7%, 2 points faster than the volume of drinks sold, because of "the timing of concentrate
shipments", it said. In other words, it shipped more concentrate than the drinks sold in the quarter used.

That is expected to reverse. Murphy said Coca-Cola now expects "slight destocking" in the fourth quarter, when
bottlers use up that stock, and the quarter also has six fewer days than a year earlier. So it expects
concentrate shipments to grow a little more slowly than volume over the whole year.

### Against what analysts expected

Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected comparable earnings per share of 88 cents and revenue of $12.92 billion,
CNBC reported. Coca-Cola reported 91 cents and $13.4 billion.

### The forecast, raised for the third time

Coca-Cola gives its forecast as growth on $3.00, its comparable earnings per share for 2025. It has raised it with
every set of results this year:

| Forecast given in | Comparable earnings per share growth | Organic revenue growth |
|---|---|---|
| February | 7% to 8% | 4% to 5% |
| April | 8% to 9% | 4% to 5% |
| July | 9% to 10% | About 5% |
| October | At least 11% | At least 5% |

Growth of at least 11% on $3.00 would be comparable earnings per share of at least $3.33 for 2026. Coca-Cola also
raised its free cash flow forecast for the year, the cash it generates after spending on equipment, to at least
$13.0 billion, from about $12.4 billion.

About 3 points of that 11% is expected to come from currency movements, Coca-Cola said. Leaving out currency and
the businesses it has bought and sold, it expects growth of at least 9%, up from 7% to 8%.

### Next year: selling the African bottler

Coca-Cola is selling its bottling business in Africa, which it expects to complete in the fourth quarter, subject to
regulatory approval. Because a bottler's revenue is large compared with its profit, the sale will shrink Coca-Cola's
revenue far more than its earnings. For 2027, it expects the sale to take about 8 points off its comparable revenue
growth and about 2 points off comparable earnings per share.

Murphy said Coca-Cola is also "exploring a potential IPO of our bottling operations in India". It will give its
forecast for 2027 when it reports fourth-quarter results.

### The tax case worth $6 billion

Coca-Cola has been fighting the US Internal Revenue Service in court since 2015 over how much of the income of its
foreign licensees should be taxed in the US. The US Tax Court sided mainly with the IRS, and in September 2024
Coca-Cola paid $6.0 billion of tax and interest for 2007 to 2009 while it appeals. It would get some or all of that
back, with interest, if it wins.

The appeals court heard the case on 25 June 2026. Coca-Cola says it is "more likely than not" to win. If it lost,
it would not get the $6.0 billion back, and it estimates the further tax and interest for 2010 to 2025 at about
$14 billion as of the end of 2025. It also estimates that losing would raise its tax rate each year by about
3.8%.

On the results call, Murphy said Coca-Cola was "continuing to judiciously manage our balance sheet" while it
waited for the decision.

### Cash and the dividend

Coca-Cola generated $11.4 billion of free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026. Its net debt was 1.3 times
its earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, Murphy said, below its target of 2 to 2.5 times.

It has paid a dividend of $0.53 a share each quarter this year. Its board declared the same again in October,
payable on 15 December, a rate of $2.12 a year, 2.3% of the share price on 28 October.

### How the shares reacted

Coca-Cola shares set a record close of $95.34 on 22 October, five days before the results. They closed at $92.70 on
26 October, and on results day, 27 October, they fell 1.6% to $91.18. The S&P 500 rose 0.6% the same day.

CNBC reported that the shares fell "on its gloomy forecast for the consumer environment". Murphy said Coca-Cola
expected consumer behaviour in 2027 "to remain largely unchanged, with value being a very important consideration
across all markets and income groups", and that the restaurant industry was "under pressure".

On 28 October the shares closed at $91.73, 3.8% below their record and up 31.2% since the end of 2025. Counting
dividends, they were up 33.8%. If 2026 ended there, it would be Coca-Cola's best year since 1996.

### How Coca-Cola shares usually react to results

We measured all 43 of Coca-Cola's results releases before this one, from February 2016 to July 2026. Coca-Cola
reports before the market opens, so we compared the close on the day it reported with the close the day before.

- The middle move, up or down, was **1.7%**.
- Only 2 of the 43 moved the shares by 5% or more, and none by 10%.
- The largest fall was **8.4%** in February 2019.
- The largest rise was **6.1%** in July 2019.
- The shares rose after 26 of the 43 and fell after 17.
- The two results before this one sent the shares up **3.9%** in April and **5.0%** in July 2026.

So a 1.6% fall is an ordinary move for Coca-Cola: 23 of the 43 earlier results moved the shares more.

### How far Coca-Cola has fallen before

Counting dividends, Coca-Cola has fallen 30% or more four times since 1962, the start of our price history. These
are measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| March 1973 | October 1974 | −68.2% | November 1982 |
| August 1987 | October 1987 | −41.2% | March 1989 |
| July 1998 | March 2003 | −55.0% | March 2011 |
| February 2020 | March 2020 | −37.0% | July 2021 |

All four got back to their record. The longest took more than 12 years from the record, and about eight years from
the low. That is a record of what happened, not a guide to what will.

### What comes next

- **The forecast.** Whether comparable earnings per share grow at least 11% in 2026, with six fewer days and some
  destocking in the fourth quarter.
- **Africa.** Whether the sale of the African bottler closes in the fourth quarter, as Coca-Cola assumes.
- **The tax case.** The appeals court's decision, which Coca-Cola is still waiting for.
- **2027.** Coca-Cola's first forecast for next year, with its fourth-quarter results.

### What this cannot tell you

share-price history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information
