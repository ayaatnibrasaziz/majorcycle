## The answer, shown under the heading

NEXTDC shares fell 41.7% on 21 October, wiping about A$2.9 billion off its value, after it cut its
forecasts for the year to June 2027. Power work its M3 Melbourne data centre needs has been
suspended after community opposition, so it cannot count on billing for that capacity this financial
year.

## Body

### The short version

- **The fall:** NEXTDC's shares were halted on Monday 19 October. When they traded again on
  Wednesday, they closed at **$5.35**, down **41.7%** from **$9.18**. It was the company's worst
  day since it listed in December 2010.
- **The money:** with 759.6 million shares on issue, that is about **A$2.9 billion** of market
  value gone in a day, from about A$7.0 billion to A$4.1 billion.
- **The cause:** Jemena, the electricity distributor that supplies M3 in West Footscray, has
  suspended part of the 66,000-volt line work needed to bring it more power. NEXTDC says Jemena
  blamed safety concerns and lost access to work sites because of community opposition, and the
  absence of council consent, and does not expect to restart before February 2027.
- **What NEXTDC changed:** it took the affected M3 contracts out of its forecast for the year to
  June 2027. Underlying EBITDA is now forecast at **A$245–265 million**, down from A$385–410 million,
  and it has put all new projects and land purchases on hold.

All figures are in Australian dollars.

### What happened on the street

The new poles are the most visible part of the work. Jemena has been replacing 7-metre wooden poles
with 15-metre poles rated for up to 66,000 volts, about 160 times the voltage previously on one
resident's street, the ABC reported in September. That resident told the ABC he learned what the
poles were for only when they went up.

In Sanderson Street, Yarraville, the suburb next to West Footscray, residents had watched the poles
go up a few blocks away. The ABC reported that on the day Jemena's crews arrived in August, residents
left their cars and bins in the street so the equipment could not be put in position, and some taped
notes to their car windows explaining why. The crews filled in the holes they had dug and left. They
had not been back by late October.

"Everyone decided this is not happening on our watch," resident Milco Bogoevski told the ABC.

Maribyrnong City Council says the poles did not need a planning permit, but Jemena needed its
consent to work in the road reserve (the footpaths and nature strips), and that the permits
it was contacted about earlier this year were not approved. The council has said it wants the lines put underground. Jemena told the ABC that
underground cables cost more and are more complex, and that its communication "could have been
better".

One caution on the story as it is often told. The council describes two sets of works in the area:
lines for the data centre in West Footscray, and upgrades to ageing power infrastructure in
Yarraville. The ABC's reporting links the Sanderson Street blockade to the power for NEXTDC's next
stage. NEXTDC's own release does not mention any street; it says Jemena suspended parts of its works
in September, citing safety concerns and lack of access to work sites arising from "community and
stakeholder opposition", and the lack of council consent.

### Why the power matters so much

A data centre is a building full of computers that companies rent. Its product is, in effect,
electricity delivered safely to those computers, so a data centre can only charge for the space its
power supply can run.

That is why NEXTDC's results in August mattered. At 30 June 2026 it had signed customers for
**740.1 megawatts** of capacity but was billing for **175.0 megawatts**. The difference, **565.1
megawatts**, more than three times what it bills today, is contracted revenue that only starts once
the capacity is built and powered. Much of the company's value rests on that backlog turning into
billing.

M3 in West Footscray is part of that backlog. NEXTDC added 42 megawatts there in the year to June
and had another 165 megawatts in progress. The ABC describes the site as about 15 metres from
homes, with planned capacity of 225 megawatts. When the next increase in its power supply slipped,
so did the date NEXTDC can bill for it.

### What NEXTDC changed on 21 October

| | Forecast in August | Forecast now |
|---|---|---|
| Net revenue, year to June 2027 | A$615–640M | A$475–500M |
| Underlying EBITDA | A$385–410M | A$245–265M |
| Capital expenditure | A$5.25–5.75B | A$4.5–5.0B |

- **Why:** the August forecast included about **A$140 million** each of net revenue and underlying
  EBITDA from the affected M3 contracts. NEXTDC has taken them out until it can estimate them
  reliably. That is about a third of the earnings forecast at the midpoints.
- **The customer:** the capacity "remains under contract, subject to the customer's rights".
  Those contracts allow credits if capacity is late, and in some circumstances let the customer
  terminate. NEXTDC is considering whether force majeure relief applies. It has not named the
  customer.
- **Spending:** all new development projects and land acquisitions are on hold "for the foreseeable
  future", and the board will look at selling some development sites it does not need soon.
- **Debt:** NEXTDC had **A$8.7 billion** of cash and undrawn facilities at 30 September and says it
  meets its covenants. It also warned that a material change to when customers start paying could
  limit how much of its A$6.7 billion of undrawn senior debt it can draw.
- **More delay:** later power increases at M3 depend on new transformers at Brooklyn Terminal
  Station, now expected later than planned, and NEXTDC no longer expects the Victorian
  government's decision on a permit for M3's next two stages until 2027, after the state election
  on 28 November.

Two prices from this year put the fall in context. In April and May, NEXTDC sold new shares to its
existing investors at **$12.70** to raise about A$1.5 billion. In September, it raised A$1.1 billion of
convertible notes against a share price of **$12.60**. On 23 October the shares were **$5.61**, more than
55% below both.

### The worst day in its history

We measured every trading day since NEXTDC listed in December 2010, close to close. Before
21 October, it had fallen 10% or more in a day only once: **13.9%** in November 2020. The 41.7% fall
is three times that.

### How far NEXTDC has fallen before

Measured from a record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it, NEXTDC has had
seven falls of 20% or more before this one, and came back from all seven:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 2011 | Sep 2011 | −30.5% | Mar 2012 |
| Mar 2012 | Jan 2013 | −27.9% | May 2013 |
| Jul 2013 | Jul 2014 | −48.4% | Apr 2016 |
| Sep 2016 | Dec 2016 | −36.5% | May 2017 |
| Jun 2018 | Nov 2018 | −29.0% | Mar 2020 |
| Mar 2020 | Mar 2020 | −23.9% | Mar 2020 |
| Nov 2020 | Oct 2022 | −40.8% | Jan 2024 |
| **Jul 2024** | **Oct 2026 (so far)** | **−71.0%** | **Not yet** |

The record close was **$18.44** on 9 July 2024. On 21 October the shares were 71.0% below it, and
69.6% below on 23 October. The deepest fall NEXTDC had come back from before was 48.4%, in 2013–14,
and that took until April 2016. So the current fall has no precedent in its own history.

This was already unusual before the halt. Our article of 15 October found NEXTDC 52.0% below its
record, past the deepest fall it had recovered from. That is a record of what happened, not a guide
to what will.

Measured on our stock page's setting, NEXTDC normally falls about **19.8%** below its one-year high
before turning. On 23 October it was **66.6%** below.

### Is this about AI?

That is the question many readers are asking, and the evidence points both ways.

- **Against a demand problem:** NEXTDC says the capacity is still under contract and construction of
  the data halls continues. Luke Laretive of Seneca Financial Solutions told SBS the episode probably
  says little about AI demand.
- **For a wider worry:** the same month, the AI data centre company Firmus withdrew its planned ASX
  listing, and on 22 October the Nasdaq fell 2.6% on what reports put down to worries about
  AI spending.
- **What is clearly new** is the risk itself: the ABC called it a dispute in a suburban street
  wiping A$3 billion off a listed company in a day, and in September the Victorian government
  announced rules to ban data centres in residential areas, with a 150-metre buffer from homes. NEXTDC's chief executive, Craig
  Scroggie, also chairs the industry body, Data Centres Australia.

### What comes next

- **3 November:** Victoria's caretaker period begins ahead of the 28 November election.
- **20 November:** NEXTDC's AGM, where it has promised the outcome of its spending review.
- **February 2027:** the earliest Jemena expects to restart the suspended works.
- **The year to June 2028:** when NEXTDC now expects the next increase in power at M3.

### What this cannot tell you

These are NEXTDC's own forecasts and Jemena's estimate, as reported by NEXTDC; both can change. The
share-price history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general
information and does not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs.
