## Body

### The short version

Arm, the British company that licenses chip designs to the companies that make the chips, reported its results for
the three months to 30 September 2026, the second quarter of its financial year, after the market closed on
4 November. In short:

- **Revenue:** $1,443 million, up 27% from $1,135 million a year earlier, its best second quarter.
- **Licences:** licence and other revenue of $773 million, up 50%.
- **Royalties:** royalty revenue of $670 million, up 8%, held back, Arm said, by the impact of memory pricing on
  the smartphone market.
- **Profit:** net income of $130 million, down from $238 million. On Arm's own adjusted measure, earnings per share
  rose 33% to $0.52.
- **The forecast:** revenue of $1.54 billion, give or take $50 million, for the three months to December.
- **The shares:** down 13.1% on 5 November, to $166.72, on a day the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5%.

All figures are in US dollars.

### The quarter against a year earlier

| | Jul–Sep 2026 | A year earlier | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $1,443M | $1,135M | +27% |
| Licence and other revenue | $773M | $515M | +50% |
| Royalty revenue | $670M | $620M | +8% |
| Operating income | $201M | $163M | +23% |
| Net income | $130M | $238M | −45% |

Arm's adjusted measure, which leaves out the cost of the shares it pays its staff in, among other items, put
operating income at $638 million, a margin of 44.2%, up from 41.1%.

Net income fell for a reason outside the business itself. A year earlier, Arm had $128 million of other
non-operating income; this quarter that line was a $2 million loss. So its income before tax was $208 million,
against $302 million.

### The royalties

Arm earns money in two ways. Companies pay a licence fee to use its designs, and then a royalty on every chip they
ship that uses them. Licences are the leading indicator; royalties are the money that follows once those chips are
in production.

This quarter the two parted company. Licence revenue grew 50%. Royalty revenue grew 8%, which Arm said was driven
by data centres but "partially offset by the impact of memory pricing on the smartphone market". Its data-centre
royalties, it said, doubled, and Arm-based chips' share of the servers at the biggest cloud companies rose from 15% in
calendar 2024 to more than 50% in calendar 2025.

Arm's explanation is timing. It called strong licensing "an important leading indicator of future royalty growth as
these new designs enter production where we expect royalty revenue to accelerate". Its annualised contract value,
which smooths out the timing of licence deals, rose 12% to $1,790 million. And royalties from cars and robots,
which Arm calls physical AI, grew 50%.

### Its customers

For the first time, Arm said, its partners shipped more than 10 billion Arm-based chips in a quarter, 1.5 billion
more than a year earlier.

- **Data centres.** Arm named Google's Axion, Amazon's Graviton and Microsoft's Cobalt chips and NVIDIA's platforms,
  and said it expects Arm's share of servers at the biggest cloud companies to pass 60% by the end of 2026. It wrote
  that "Amazon recently reported that Graviton revenue commitments increased more than 20x year over year".
- **PCs.** Arm-based PCs, it said, are now about 20% of global shipments.
- **Phones.** It named OPPO and vivo as adopting its latest phone designs, and Xiaomi its newest graphics chip.

### Against what analysts expected

Before the results, analysts had expected revenue of $1.38 billion and adjusted earnings of $0.47 a share,
according to the Motley Fool. Arm reported $1,443 million and $0.52. For the three months to December, Arm's
forecast of $1.54 billion compared with an analysts' consensus of $1.53 billion, according to Proactive.

Coverage of the fall focused on the royalties. The Motley Fool wrote: "Chief among the red flags behind today's
sizable sell-off is Arm Holdings' Q2 royalty revenue."

### Its new chip business

Arm has started selling a chip of its own, the Arm AGI CPU, rather than only licensing designs. It said it had
purchase orders from multiple customers extending through calendar 2027, with first commercial deliveries on track
for the last quarter of its financial year, January to March 2027, and that its confidence in "achieving up to $2 billion in Arm AGI CPU revenue
over FY27–28" had increased.

### Profit, cash and the forecast

Arm keeps almost all of the money it earns after the cost of what it sells: its gross margin was 97.1%. It spent
$857 million on research and development, up 24%. Cash from its operations fell to $207 million, from $567 million
a year earlier, and its free cash flow over the past 12 months was $1,096 million. At 30 September it held
$3,621 million in cash and short-term investments.

For the three months to December, Arm forecast revenue of $1.54 billion, give or take $50 million, and adjusted
earnings of $0.56 a share, give or take $0.04.

### What its chief executive said

In Arm's letter to shareholders, chief executive Rene Haas said: "Our IP business continues to grow, with 50%
licensing growth demonstrating the increasing investment in the power efficient Arm compute platform which will
drive even higher royalty growth in the future."

He added: "Arm is becoming the compute platform of choice as AI transforms every market."

These are Arm's views of its own business, not ours.

### How the shares reacted

Arm closed at $191.80 on 4 November. It released its results after the market closed, and on 5 November the shares
were as low as $163.13, before closing at $166.72, down 13.1%.

It was a bad day for the whole market. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% and the S&P 500 4.4%.

That leaves Arm 62.1% below its record close of $439.46, set on 18 June 2026, though still up 52.5% this year.

### How Arm shares usually react to results

We measured all 12 quarterly results Arm released from November 2023, soon after it listed in September 2023, to
July 2026, comparing the close on the day of each release with the close the next day.

- The middle move, up or down, was **5.9%**.
- Eight of the 12 moved the shares 5% or more, and four 10% or more.
- The largest fall was **15.7%** in July 2024.
- The largest rise was **47.9%** in February 2024.
- The shares rose after 4 of the 12 and fell after 8.

So this was Arm's third-largest fall after results since it listed.

### How far Arm has fallen before

Arm's shares have fallen 20% or more five times since they listed, counting this one. These are measured from a
record close to the lowest close before the shares got back to it:

| Record close | Lowest point | Fall | Back at the record |
|---|---|---|---|
| September 2023 | October 2023 | −24.7% | November 2023 |
| February 2024 | April 2024 | −41.5% | June 2024 |
| July 2024 | April 2025 | −54.0% | April 2026 |
| June 2026 | June 2026 | −25.4% | June 2026 |
| June 2026 | November 2026 | −62.1% | Not yet |

The four earlier falls all got back to their record, the longest after almost two years. That is a record of what
happened, not a guide to what will.

### Legal matters

Arm is in two court cases with the chipmaker Qualcomm. In the first, which Arm brought in 2022, the court ruled in
Qualcomm's favour in September 2025, and Arm has appealed.

In the second, brought by Qualcomm in 2024, a jury found in October 2026 for Arm on four claims and for Qualcomm on
two, awarding Qualcomm nominal damages. The court has yet to decide whether Qualcomm can withhold certain royalties
from Arm. Arm said it disagrees with Qualcomm's claims and intends "to vigorously defend against them".

### What comes next

- **Royalties.** Whether smartphone royalties recover as memory prices settle.
- **The forecast.** Whether revenue for the three months to December lands near the $1.54 billion Arm expects.
- **Its own chips.** Whether the first commercial deliveries of the Arm AGI CPU arrive on time.

history is a record of what happened before, not a prediction. This article is general information and does not
